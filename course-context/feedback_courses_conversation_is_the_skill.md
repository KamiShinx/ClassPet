Ben, 24 Sep: "emphasize in the prompting section to the core how important it is to have conversations like the
ones we're having, because most kids i know that use ai to build stuff just tell it 'תבנה לי את זה' 'זה לא עובד'.
they never do specifics or cohesively explain what they want or even know what they want. this is why i also want
to deeply implement the game design thing, the character design thing, etc."

**Why:** kids fail with AI because they don't know or can't articulate what they want; design work is the cure.

**How to apply (both [[project-make-webapp-course]] and [[project-make-minecraft-course]]):** the course spine is
design → specific description → conversation → specific bug report. (1) Design card first (mechanic, character,
level) — the filled card IS the prompt; implement the game-design + character-design research deeply. (2) Show
vague vs specific prompts side by side in every lesson. (3) Talk, don't command: "ask me 5 questions first",
"suggest 3 options", "what's missing in my plan"; first answer = draft. (4) Never "זה לא עובד": bug report template
(what I did / expected / happened / error or screenshot). (5) Annotated real conversations as models, incl.
pushback ("no, that's not what I meant", "change only X"). Spoon-feed the mechanics, not the ideas.

**13 Oct 2026, Ben on web-app meeting 1 (after calling v2 "just copy paste and create a copy of a google sheet",
slides "underwhelming", Hebrew "suck"):** "we need to do the same like minecraft. coding becomes so easy/unrelevant
that they just need to learn the design aspect and planning aspects, and learn how thing work." Also: "not 'watch

educational". **How to apply:** every lesson's weight = a design talk (interactive slides the teacher clicks while

(My reading: the plan the kid writes should map onto the system's parts, e.g. see / do / remember = page / server / Sheet.)

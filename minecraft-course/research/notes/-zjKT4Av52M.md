# 66 Minutes of Cool Game Mechanics (Game Design Library, 66 min)

**What it is:** A rapid-fire compilation, ~60 mechanics from ~60 different games in ~66 minutes (one YouTube caption paragraph per game, roughly 45-90s each). No connecting theme beyond "this one thing this game does is cool."

**Substance:** substantive. Every entry names a specific, checkable mechanic and usually says *why* it works (what problem it solves, what tension it creates), not just "this game is cool." Almost no padding — it's pure content density, which is itself a problem for a 65-min class (see caveats). Source is YouTube auto-captions (no punctuation in the raw file, cleaned up here); a few names are likely mis-heard (e.g. "Kern" for climbing game **Cairn** — confirmed by the on-screen tag at 36:15).

## What the frames add
Everything — this is a screen-recording compilation with zero talking head, so the sheets are the only way to see UI layouts, not just hear them described. Specific things the audio alone doesn't convey: the exact look of Syntopia's dual overworld/hell maps (4:45-6:45); Titanium Core's actual match-3 board layout (20:34-22:00); the Inscryption death-card text ("cost of 1 blood from the cat", "bifurcated strike from mantis", 1:01:30-1:01:45); Treasure of Rudras' full letter-to-element lookup table (10:15-10:45 in video 2, referenced conceptually here); the Eternal Life of Goldman's literal 3-part cane UI (33:15-34:00); and just how UI-dense games like Marathon, Bag of Dreams and Top Deck Automat are (their "one cool mechanic" is inseparable from a busy menu screen a kid would need to design too, not just code).

## For our class (11-13, Minecraft mod, Gemini writes the code)
### Becomes something kids do
- **"Design a tool with a tradeoff" worksheet (30 min)**: pick one MC-yes item mechanic above (Donkey Kong Bananza smash-radius tool, Pinnacle Point resonator, Ball Pit returning-boomerang, Spilled's 3-stat upgrade tool) and fill in: what it does, what it costs you, what happens if you misuse it. Ends with a one-paragraph spec Gemini could build.
- **"Boss room" worksheet (30 min)**: combine Hades II's %-based wave trigger + Left 4 Dead 2's single-target-aggro special + WildGate's shared "everyone loses" countdown into one boss fight kids design on paper before touching Antigravity.
- **"Death changes you" worksheet (20 min)**: model Inscryption's death card — when you die, you get a small permanent reward built from what killed you (an enchant, a resistance, a trinket). Good advancement-system spec.

### Survives the move to Minecraft?
The item/mob mechanics marked "yes" above survive well because they're status-effect, NBT-tag, or spawn-condition logic — exactly what NeoForge + Gemini are good at, and exactly what Minecraft already has primitives for (potions, enchantments, mob AI goals, structure blocks). The ones marked "no" mostly fail for one of three reasons: they need a bespoke UI/menu system (Heaven Does Not Respond, Marathon's sell filters, Nutmeg's card hand), they need real-time multiplayer sync (Slay the Spire 2, Split Fiction's timed catch), or they need physics/animation Minecraft's engine doesn't do (Mirror's Edge momentum, Baby Steps' per-limb walking, Kaput Mortem's split camera/hand). That 3-way split is worth teaching explicitly as a filter, not just applying silently.

### Doesn't transfer
Detective/dialogue games (Viola, Paradise Killer) — need branching narrative writing, which CONTEXT.md already flags as a "novel's length" problem. Card-battler economy games (Slay the Spire 2, Nutmeg, Top Deck Automat, Bag of Dreams) — the whole appeal is a metagame of dozens of cards, not a single mechanic; a mod could reference ONE idea from these (e.g. Bag of Dreams' corruption meter) but not the genre. Anything requiring custom camera/physics rigs (Future Cop, Kaput Mortem, Baby Steps) is a multi-month engineering problem even for an experienced modder, let alone a first-year kid + Gemini.

## Honest caveats
- 60 mechanics in 66 minutes is too dense to show a class in one sitting — even as an "inspiration deck," this needs to be pre-filtered by the teacher into maybe 8-10 cards before kids see it, matching the "mechanic cards" deliverable below.
- The video's claims are mostly descriptive ("here's what the game does") rather than evaluative, so there's little here to fact-check or disagree with — my main editorializing is the MC yes/no calls, which are my own extrapolation, not the video's.
- A few mechanics (Words of Vengeance, Absalom, Skate Story) are covered in under 40 seconds each with no gameplay proof beyond a few seconds of footage — treat those as thinner/less-verified than the ones with 60-90s of coverage.

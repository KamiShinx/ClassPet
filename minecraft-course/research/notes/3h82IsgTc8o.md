# Building Universes for fun - where do you start? (Joriam Ramos, 13.8 min)

**What it is:** A talking-head essay on how to begin worldbuilding from a creative-writing angle: pick a small scale and one target emotion, then freewrite a short scene.
**Substance:** mixed — the title promises a "where do you start" procedure, but roughly the first third of the runtime is general creative-writing philosophy (not worldbuilding-specific), and only one concrete method is actually given.

## Ideas, in the video's order
- [0:00:00] Framing question: where do you start, big or small? Claims "art has no inherent rules" (generic).
- [0:00:55]-[0:02:57] "Joy of writing" tangent: any scene can be approached from many angles; choosing the focus is the craft (generic writing craft, not worldbuilding-specific).
- [0:02:57]-[0:04:39] "Good writing" = intentionally picking an emotion you want the reader to feel and crafting toward it (generic craft principle).
- [0:04:39] Reframe: worldbuilding is a subset of writing; you don't need a whole universe, just a strategic part, and readers fill the gaps (independently echoes the "world builder's disease" warning in the Stoneworks and Sins videos).
- [0:05:54] Core technique #1: pick a sense of scale first — planet, country, town, or a single room. Bigger doesn't mean more detailed; a single room can be explored more richly than a galaxy.
- [0:07:48]-[0:08:28] Core technique #2: pick ONE emotion/idea that matters to you as the entry point (his example: "cuteness"), and ask what piece of the world could evoke that state.
- [0:09:05]-[0:12:05] Worked example: a 20-minute freewrite of a talking-toaster short story built from "kitchen" (scale) + "cuteness" (emotion), then extrapolates further world details (cars, jobs, schools) from that one scene.
- [0:12:41] The point is to leave the reader with a vibe and springboard ideas, not a complete setting.

## For our class (11-13, Minecraft mod, Gemini writes the code)
### Becomes something kids do
- **"Scale + one feeling" starter card** (10 min): before building anything, kid picks (a) a scale — one block, one room, one structure, one creature, never "the whole world" — and (b) one feeling they want a player to have there (scared, cozy, proud, silly). Direct rescale of his method from "story" to "build/mob."
- **"One-room world" build exercise**: build ONE room that expresses one chosen feeling and nothing else — good as a week-1 orientation task in Minecraft itself.

### Survives the move to Minecraft?
The "pick scale small, not the whole universe" instinct maps perfectly onto Minecraft — you literally cannot build a galaxy in 65 minutes, but you absolutely can over-detail one room. "Pick a feeling first" also survives, translating into environmental storytelling (lighting, mob placement, block choice). His actual demonstration, however, is a piece of prose fiction (the toaster story), which does not transfer — the class isn't teaching short-story writing, and the video's real craft is sentence-level writing choices.

### Doesn't transfer
The entire "joy of writing"/emotional-bait framework (reader psychology, prose technique, "conductor of somebody's internal orchestra") is literary craft aimed at readers of prose — there's no class equivalent of "the reader," and there's no time for 11-13-year-olds to learn short-story writing. The worked toaster example is charming but not something to show or imitate directly.

## Honest caveats
Despite the title promising "where do you start," the video spends real time on general writing philosophy before it delivers its one concrete method — it doesn't fully deliver on its own premise. This is the weakest video in the batch for the class's specific "usable starting procedure" question, despite having the most on-the-nose title match.

# Experimental Kart Racers - Sonic, Kirby, Snowboard Kids & More (Design Doc, 16.4 min)

**What it is:** Five case studies of kart racers that broke the Mario Kart formula: Team Sonic Racing (team-shared meters/items), Crash Tag Team Racing (a broken "clash" mechanic), Kirby Air Ride (one-button controls), Snowboard Kids (snowboard-physics racing + lift-based laps), Speedrunners (platformer-racer hybrid).
**Substance:** mixed: well-argued per game, but two sponsor reads and it's still a racing-genre survey - low overlap with a Minecraft mod class.

## Ideas, in the video's order (brief - low relevance)
- [0:01:42]-[0:04:28] Team Sonic Racing: shared team meter and item-passing mechanics built teamwork into the core loop rather than bolting it onto a solo mode. (Interesting multiplayer-cooperation idea; no equivalent verb in a single-player-per-kid Minecraft mod.)
- [0:04:59]-[0:06:35] Crash Tag Team Racing's "clash" mechanic: a broken exploit (attach to a leader's car and coast to the win) presented as a cautionary tale of an unplaytested mechanic. (generic "playtest your exploits" lesson)
- [0:06:35]-[0:08:44] Kirby Air Ride: everything (braking, drifting, item-copy, ability activation) runs through ONE button, made legible by being context-sensitive to what's nearby. Same "versatile verb" idea already logged from Batch I ("The Secret of Mario's Jump"), just a second data point.
- [0:09:14]-[0:11:58] Snowboard Kids: reskins racing mechanics around snowboard physics (weight-shifting, losing your edge) and reworks laps into a "ride the lift back up" positioning mini-game - theme-driven mechanic redesign, but racing-specific.
- [0:12:32]-[0:14:47] Speedrunners: swaps lap-based racing for a shared-screen elimination format with platforming - shows a genre's core loop can be rebuilt around a different pacing structure.

## For our class (11-13, Minecraft mod, Gemini writes the code)
### Becomes something kids do
Nothing new. The "one button, context-sensitive" idea (Kirby Air Ride) is the only piece with any Minecraft-shaped potential, and it's a straight repeat of Batch I's "versatile verbs" idea - don't build a second worksheet around it.

### Survives the move to Minecraft?
Team-based shared meters (Team Sonic Racing) don't fit an 8-kid class where each kid builds their own solo mod - there's no shared multiplayer party structure to hook into.

### Doesn't transfer
Kart/vehicle physics, lap structures, and item-box balancing across five racing games - none of this maps onto Minecraft's item/mob/block/structure toolkit. Racing as a genre isn't buildable as a "NeoForge mod mechanic" in any of the senses the class cares about.

As flagged by the batch theme, this is low relevance and the note reflects that honestly - one repeated idea (versatile single-button verb) is worth a mention, nothing here is worth a new worksheet.

# Final Fantasy IX - How to Pace a Game (Design Doc, 11.7 min)

**What it is:** Video essay on FFIX's narrative pacing (fast opening, no exposition dump) and its AP/ability system (learn skills from equipped gear over time).
**Substance:** mixed: the narrative-pacing half is competent but is book/TV craft (character introductions, plot doled out per location), not a game mechanic. The AP-system half is the one genuinely game-y idea and it's covered in under 2 minutes.

## Ideas, in the video's order
- [3:24] Narrative pacing = plot events chosen and timed, not raw content; bad pacing (e.g. a 2-hour prisoner-cart ride) bores players (generic storytelling craft, not Minecraft-relevant).
- [4:29] FFIX introduces its full main cast within the first hour via short, fast-cut character segments rather than one long intro (generic writing technique).
- [7:09] "30 seconds of fun" (Bungie's Jaime Griesemer): a game's core moment-to-moment loop (here: sneak up, grenade, clean up) has to be fun on repeat, because everything else is a variation layered on top of it (a genuinely useful naming of a design idea, though not new to game-design literacy).
- [8:47] FFIX's AP system: every weapon/armor piece teaches an ability as you fight while wearing it; once learned, you keep the ability even after unequipping. Swapping gear mid-dungeon gives you an immediate "test run" of a new move.
- [9:49] Because different equipped items' AP bars fill at different rates, almost every battle unlocks *something*, which the video says keeps the drip of small progress constant and stops the game "bogging down" (this is an assertion about pacing feel, not measured).

## For our class (11-13, Minecraft mod, Gemini writes the code)
### Becomes something kids do
- **"Learn it by wearing it" tooltip (concept only, not a worksheet)**: when specifying a custom item/armor piece to Gemini, a kid could ask for an enchantment-like effect that only unlocks permanently after the item has been worn/used N times — a NeoForge-buildable variant of FFIX's AP system. Worth one line in a tooltip about item design, not a full activity on its own.

### Survives the move to Minecraft?
The "gear teaches you a permanent ability after enough use" pattern is buildable as a custom enchantment/advancement trigger in NeoForge (track use-count via player NBT/capability, grant an effect once a threshold is hit). The "30 seconds of fun" framing is a decent one-line design lens for judging any Minecraft mob/item loop, but it's not specific to this video — it's general game-design vocabulary.

### Doesn't transfer
The narrative-pacing content (character intro timing, avoiding exposition dumps, disk/story structure) is novelist/screenwriter craft for a 10+ hour scripted RPG. It belongs to worldbuilding lore writing at most, not to a game-mechanics worksheet, and even there it's generic ("introduce characters fast, don't infodump").

## Honest caveats
This video is mostly irrelevant to the class as flagged in the batch brief.

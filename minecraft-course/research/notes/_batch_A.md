# Batch A summary — Character Design (6 videos)

## Where the videos agree (high confidence — build the curriculum's spine here)
**Silhouette is king.** All six videos independently land on this as the #1 rule: Pixel Pete and Draw like a Sir both show the classic solid-black cartoon-silhouette recognition test; Map Crow and KeshArt both test their own sketches by filling them solid black; Proko ranks silhouette/proportion/scale as literally the first thing a viewer notices, before face or color. This is the single most cross-validated idea in the batch and should be the course's first, non-negotiable design rule — and it happens to matter MORE in Minecraft than in illustration, since a cube mob at typical play distance is read almost entirely by outline.

**Limit and plan the color palette.** Pixel Pete (2-4 colors, cites Sonic/Kirby/Pac-Man), Draw like a Sir (2-3 dominant + one "pop" complementary color, muted not max-saturated), and KeshArt (named Primary/Complementary/Necessary/Accent system) all converge on the same rule from different angles. This maps unusually well onto a 16x16 pixel texture, which already forces color economy — teach KeshArt's 4-box system as the worksheet, it's the most concrete.

**Design from function/personality, not decoration.** Pixel Pete ("one interesting trait tied to backstory"), Map Crow (symbol-inventory word list before drawing), Draw like a Sir (Step 0: personality before body), Proko (priority ranking driven by defined backstory), and KeshArt (one-word brief, "give a man a fish" framing) all say the same thing: decide what the character IS and DOES before touching shape. This is also the best bridge to the course's other creative strand — a mob's design should follow from its lore/behavior, tying character design directly to worldbuilding and to the modded mechanic Gemini will build.

**One dominant trait, not five.** Pixel Pete states it directly ("just need one trait that's memorable"); Proko's "which trait carries the personality" exercise is the sophisticated version of the same idea. Good antidote to kids piling on unrelated cool stuff.

**Base shape/proportion before detail.** Draw like a Sir (Step 1 before Step 2), Map Crow (rough gesture without erasing, no early detail), and KeshArt (explicitly names his own mistake: added detail before fixing the base) all agree. Worth enforcing as a literal workflow gate in Blockbench sessions.

## Where they add up to more than any one video (combine, don't pick one)
No real videos contradict each other on substance, but they complement: Map Crow's random d10 table + symbol-inventory list and Proko's six named idea-generation techniques (visual mix, what-if, reverse, scale, genre fusion, anachronism) together make a strong, fast, drawing-free "stuck for ideas" toolkit — better combined than either alone. Proko's iconography demo (2-3 items reskin one base figure into cowboy/pirate/agent/soldier) is the single most literally reusable classroom exercise in the batch precisely because it needs zero drawing skill and models exactly what a kid will do choosing which cubes/textures to add.

## The one real contradiction — flag it explicitly
Map Crow calls T-poses/turnarounds "basically worthless" for illustration and refuses to use them. This directly conflicts with what a Minecraft mod actually needs: Blockbench models are built around a clear rest pose, which is much closer to a T-pose/turnaround than to Map Crow's dynamic gesture-first approach. Don't import this advice — tell kids a clean rest pose is a feature of the medium, not a cop-out.

## Ideas to cut or heavily downweight
- **pikat's full research pipeline** (Milanote boards, museum deep-dives, multi-day process) is the most intellectually rigorous idea in the batch but is built for someone spending weeks on one illustration — utterly incompatible with 65-70 min/week across 8 kids. Keep only the compressed version: "what does it eat/where does it live/what's its power, and does the design show that?"
- **Fine physique/anatomy distinction** (Draw like a Sir's slim/lean/bulky muscle rendering, Proko's body-exposure discussion) — mostly lost at cube resolution and not age-appropriate to dwell on.
- **All inking/rendering/shading craft** (KeshArt's brush-pen technique, Draw like a Sir's manga contrast rules, Map Crow's ruler-for-straight-edges) — zero Blockbench equivalent; cut entirely.
- **Exaggeration-for-charm as a 2D proportion trick** (Pixel Pete's oversized bomb) — appealing idea but risks looking broken rather than stylish in cubes without real testing; treat as experimental, not a rule.
- **Real-world military/subculture iconography specifics** (Proko's PMC-vs-mercenary-vs-security-guard chain) — the CONCEPT (few items = archetype) is gold, the specific examples are adult and shouldn't be shown as-is.

## Overall recommendation
Anchor the character-design unit on three testable, drawing-optional activities that all six videos support: (1) a silhouette black-fill test, (2) a 4-box color worksheet, (3) a "why does this feature exist" function check tied to the mob's lore/behavior. Everything else in this batch is either reinforcement of those three ideas or illustration craft that doesn't survive the move to cubes.

# Batch G — Sanderson's BYU lectures on character and magic systems

Four 2025 BYU "Writing Science Fiction and Fantasy" lectures: #5 Proactive/Relatable/Capable characters
(G-6z7JqvDoE, 70 min), #6 Customizing your Character / motivation-personality-values (NHTYFX24BgQ, 76 min),
#6.5 Types of Character Arcs, a web Q&A (-QjdOgbylGQ, 32 min), and #7 Sanderson's Laws of Magic
(3Y9p53C1lP4, 74 min). All four are pure talking-head lectures with static whiteboards; visuals add
nothing beyond pacing — every idea below comes from the transcripts.

## Strongest ideas across the batch

1. **Sanderson's Laws of Magic ARE game-mechanic design, almost unmodified.** The standout, exactly as
   the brief predicted. Law 1 (magic can only satisfyingly resolve problems the reader/player already
   understands) maps to: mod mechanics must be learnable before they can fairly decide a fight. Law 2
   (limitations are more interesting than powers) maps to game balance directly. His four categories —
   cost, flaw, limitation, hindrance [3Y9p53C1lP4, 0:59:12] — are a ready-made worksheet for specifying
   any item/ability/boss mechanic, and are also unambiguous enough to hand to Gemini as a spec. This is
   the one place where "craft term" and "technical spec" are nearly the same document.
2. **Law 3 ("expand what you have before adding something new") is a scope-control tool for the course
   itself** [3Y9p53C1lP4, 1:09:01]. With 8 kids each free to invent unlimited systems over 20 weeks,
   "have I already got this? can I deepen it?" is a useful recurring check — the Daggerfall-vs-Skyrim
   point ("an ocean an inch deep" [1:11:49]) that a wide roster of shallow mobs is worse than a few
   well-realized ones.
3. **Motivation as a design spec for mob AI.** "What does the character want, even if it's small"
   [NHTYFX24BgQ 0:06:01; G-6z7JqvDoE 0:20:38] converts almost losslessly into the goal spec a NeoForge
   mob AI needs. Strongest transfer point in the character lectures because it's motivation-as-behaviour,
   not writing craft — a mob with no dialogue can still embody it.
4. **"Foreshadow before you resolve," repeated across both magic and character material**
   [3Y9p53C1lP4 0:22:22 Helm's Deep; -QjdOgbylGQ 0:10:10 "hang a lantern on it"]. A big payoff (a boss's
   weak point, a risky decision paying off) needs an earlier, safe demonstration or it reads as
   arbitrary. Two videos converging on this independently is a sign it's real, not a personal tic.
5. **One trait, not a pile of them, per mob/NPC.** The "10% exaggeration, not 100%" Cohen Brothers point
   [NHTYFX24BgQ 1:00:24] and "quirk that reinforces motivation vs. quirk for quirk's sake"
   [NHTYFX24BgQ 0:58:44] both land the same lesson: one legible, reinforcing behavioural tell beats
   several decorative ones — and matches Minecraft's silhouette-driven, low-res look from CONTEXT.md.
6. **Iconic vs. arcing, as mob-roster triage.** All three character videos distinguish characters needing
   a personal arc from "iconic" ones that don't [NHTYFX24BgQ 0:02:08; -QjdOgbylGQ 0:05:54, 0:19:21].
   Translated: most mobs are "iconic" (a zombie is always a zombie); only a few signature bosses/NPCs
   deserve arc-design time (phase changes, escalating behaviour) — a useful check against over-speccing
   every mob in a 65-minute session.
7. **The three-slider (Proactive/Relatable/Capable) card** [G-6z7JqvDoE 0:09:05] is reusable as a mob
   design sheet, with "relatable" reduced to "one observable behavioural tell" rather than anything
   resembling audience sympathy-building (see cuts, below).

## Where videos agree or contradict

- **Strong agreement:** lecture #5's "small thing before the big thing" [0:26:02] and lecture #7's "seed
  a demonstration before the resolution needs it" [0:22:22, 0:28:19] are the same principle, reinforced
  independently across a character and a worldbuilding lecture — the most load-bearing idea in the batch.
- **Structural agreement, weak transfer:** #6 and #6.5 both warn a main character/system needs the same
  texture as side characters or it goes flat [-QjdOgbylGQ 0:23:44, "Harry Potter problem"]. Real craft,
  but our class has no single "main character" — the player's own kit is the closest analogue.
- **Minor tension, actually useful:** #5 sells Proactive/Relatable/Capable as *the* framework for
  characters, while #7 says worldbuilding/systems are "the least important of the three" story legs
  [3Y9p53C1lP4 0:03:50]. Read together, this supports Ben's instinct that character/lore alone isn't
  enough — Sanderson himself says strong worldbuilding with weak plot/character is just an encyclopedia
  entry.

## Ideas to cut

- NHTYFX24BgQ's back third (romance pacing, indie-publishing plug, paid sensitivity readers, dialect
  craft) — adult-novelist/publishing content with no path into an 11-13 Minecraft class.
- The antihero/grimdark moral-complexity discussion [G-6z7JqvDoE 0:49:10–0:54:40] — needs a novel's worth
  of sustained narrative our format doesn't have.
- Series/sequel arc-pacing, multi-viewpoint selection, "setting as its own character"
  [-QjdOgbylGQ 0:05:54 onward] — real craft, belongs (if anywhere) in a worldbuilding batch, and doesn't
  survive translation into mod mechanics regardless.
- "Relatable" as Sanderson defines it (viewpoint sympathy, self-aware flaws, moral alignment) mostly
  doesn't transfer to a non-speaking mob — keep only "one observable behavioural tell," drop the dialogue
  and viewpoint apparatus.
- Sanderson's publishing anecdotes (Mistborn's near-flop, cover redesigns) [3Y9p53C1lP4 0:53:57–0:56:36]
  — engaging, zero design content.

## Bottom line

Ben suspected the character/worldbuilding list might not be enough and that game-design content would
also be needed. This batch confirms it: the character lectures give real but heavily-filtered material
for mob/boss *design flavor* (motivation, one strong trait, iconic-vs-arc triage), while the actual
"what makes mechanics fun" content comes almost entirely from the magic-systems lecture alone — and even
that mapping is our own translation, since Sanderson never once mentions games. The batch is not a
substitute for dedicated game-design sources, but Law 2's cost/flaw/limitation/hindrance framework is the
strongest single piece of transferable content in this research project so far.

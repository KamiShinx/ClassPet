# Publishing Lectures: Relevance Audit

These four Brandon Sanderson lectures (BYU 2025) are about the *publishing business* — contracts, agents, royalties, query letters, pitching to agents and editors. **Almost nothing transfers to a Minecraft mod course for 11-13-year-olds.**

## Lecture #10: How Publishing Works (75 min)
Industry history, consolidation, ebook/audiobook disruptions, agent roles, royalty percentages, editor responsibilities, contract basics. **Nothing relevant.** All machinery and economics of selling books to traditional publishers.

## Lecture #11: What Publishing Does For You (74 min)
How to break into traditional publishing: agents, query letters, elevator pitches, conventions, comp titles, contract red flags (world rights, film rights, non-compete). **Nothing relevant.** Entirely about the professional publication funnel.

## Lecture #11.5: Demystifying the Publishing Process (31 min)
Q&A on indie vs. traditional, novellas, foreign markets, agent commission. **Nothing relevant.** More publishing business.

## Lecture #12: An Ending and A Beginning (73 min)
Mostly Q&A on breaking in, platform building, YA vs. middle grade, Kickstarter fulfilment, AI art ethics. **One thin exception:**

Revision and feedback [0:08:23–0:10:03]: Learning to revise by testing feedback, building instincts, trying revision strategies. Acknowledges some authors hate beta readers; others need them. "Best way to learn revision is trial and error." This is generic but *useful* — can be repurposed as "test your builds with others and see what works."

Persistence/finishing [1:09:50–1:12:09]: "I made that decision I was going to keep writing even if I never sold a book... You have to be somebody willing to write even if you never publish." Also: "If you finish a novel you are among the elite." This *applies* to Minecraft mods (finish what you start), but is not specific to game design and won't land with kids as powerfully as examples from their own medium.

## Assessment

These lectures are weaponized for novelists pursuing traditional publishing contracts. Even Sanderson's career stories assume agents, editors, agents, bestseller lists, Hollywood. The only usable bite is that revision + feedback works, and finishing matters—true for any creative project but not shaped for how kids actually build mods (in-engine iteration, friend testing, rapid rebuild cycles). **Not a good source for course content.**

# Batch K summary — "intro to designing mechanics" (the teacher's own picks)

Eight videos Ben picked himself, split into tiers: two genuinely strong essays (Extra Credits, GMTK), two thin
generic explainers from one small channel (RookieDev), one solid-but-recycled MDA recap, one long academic essay
too abstract for 11-13-year-olds, one solo board-game dev vlog with a few good fragments, and one Godot coding
tutorial barely about mechanics at all. Ben's "beginner tutorials" label undersells the unevenness — the two
best videos beat several from the earlier, higher-effort batches, while half this batch adds nothing beyond
what the class already has from MDA/GMTK.

## Do the short beginner videos teach anything beyond earlier GMTK/MDA material?
Mostly no. `7oBHqVQ-JLA` re-teaches MDA with no new technique. `pmCDENTengA`/`X81443xLeh8` (RookieDev Parts 1-2)
teach a core/secondary/emergent taxonomy and a "feeling before mechanic" ordering — real but thin, no technique
the earlier checklists don't cover better. Two videos ARE genuinely new: Extra Credits' `sLXLlJ7FhJU`
(hidden-mechanic perception tricks) and GMTK's `hbzGO_Qonu0` (pacing pillars, mystery, long-term-goal
structure) — neither overlaps MDA or the earlier checklists, both convert into buildable worksheets.

## The 6 strongest ideas across this batch
1. **Hidden mechanics that fudge fairness, not truth** (`sLXLlJ7FhJU` [0:02:15]-[0:04:00]): streak-breaking on
   stated odds, mercy-invincibility windows, coyote-time forgiveness — a one-variable tweak to an existing
   Minecraft system, and the most directly buildable idea in the batch.
2. **Pacing pillars + intensity modulation** (`hbzGO_Qonu0` [0:01:08]-[0:02:47]): naming a mod's 3-4 activity
   types and checking none runs too long unbroken. Sequencing across a session, not classifying one mechanic.
3. **Mystery over reveal** (`hbzGO_Qonu0` [0:03:49]-[0:04:55], echoed independently by `qIiViwg_tTg`
   [0:01:30]-[0:02:33]'s empty-hex-map hook). Two unrelated channels converge: a visible-but-inaccessible thing
   sustains engagement longer than delivering the reward.
4. **Long-term goal + milestones + exponential loop** (`hbzGO_Qonu0` [0:05:31]-[0:07:36]): Stardew/Factorio
   structure maps onto a custom ore-to-tool chain, and answers the class's own "insane lore and mechanics by
   year's end" goal.
5. **Personal, non-competitive victory conditions** (`qIiViwg_tTg` [0:09:55]-[0:11:28]): each player defines
   their own goal at setup, mapping onto Minecraft's existing advancement system with no new engine work.
6. **"What does this make the player feel, on purpose?"** (`KkdPxZbUNSw` [0:00:00]-[0:01:39], heavily stripped
   down). The one sentence worth salvaging from a 24-minute essay whose examples need hours of authored
   narrative a solo kid's mod can't replicate.

## Where videos agree, contradict, or add nothing
- **Independent agreement**: `hbzGO_Qonu0` and `qIiViwg_tTg` both land on "an unresolved mystery beats a
  delivered reward," from unrelated contexts (AAA console games vs. a hobbyist board game).
- **Redundant, not contradictory**: `7oBHqVQ-JLA` restates MDA; the RookieDev pair restates "iterate, balance,
  playtest" as a taxonomy. Nothing contradicts earlier material, it just doesn't add to it.
- **Off-target, not wrong**: `j3f8xOv2Tpg` is a Godot coding tutorial wearing a "make mechanics" title — only a
  planning habit (PPCAR) and a debugging mindset transfer; the code and the rhythm mechanic don't, since Gemini
  writes the code in this class.
- **Too abstract, not useless**: `KkdPxZbUNSw` is the best-researched video here and fails the class's filter
  questions almost entirely — "well-made" and "usable for 11-13-year-olds" are different axes.

## What I'd cut
- Both RookieDev videos as standalone activities — lift one sentence ("feeling before mechanic") only.
- `KkdPxZbUNSw`'s middle 15 minutes (semiotic readings, Panksepp's emotion circuits, "three-lens" framework) —
  real film-critical content, zero one-page-spec value here.
- `j3f8xOv2Tpg`'s code walkthrough and sponsor segment — keep only the PPCAR planning habit.
- `qIiViwg_tTg`'s hex-tile/dice mechanics as literal content — keep only what generalizes.

## Mechanic cards
1. **Coyote-time movement**: "A short window to jump back after stepping off a ledge — how forgiving should
   movement near your structure feel?" (`sLXLlJ7FhJU` [0:05:34])
2. **Mercy hit**: "A killing blow instead leaves you at 1 HP with brief invincibility: how do you keep this from
   feeling like cheating?" (`sLXLlJ7FhJU` [0:03:24]-[0:04:00])
3. **Honest-feeling odds**: "Your enchant says '30% chance to poison' but players get angry after 2 misses in a
   row: do you quietly raise the odds after a miss streak?" (`sLXLlJ7FhJU` [0:02:15]-[0:02:50])
4. **Locked door, no reward yet**: "A sealed structure appears in week 3; the item that opens it isn't buildable
   until week 8: what's behind it?" (`hbzGO_Qonu0` [0:03:49]-[0:04:55])
5. **Self-feeding resource loop**: "A custom ore lets you build a tool that gathers more of itself automatically:
   what stops it breaking the economy?" (`hbzGO_Qonu0` [0:07:05]-[0:07:36])
6. **Pick-your-own-ending advancement**: "Let the player choose their own goal at the start instead of one fixed
   boss-kill win: how does a custom advancement track three personal goals?" (`qIiViwg_tTg` [0:09:55]-[0:11:28])
7. **Shared-health companion**: "A tamed mob's health is secretly tied to yours — hurting it hurts you: what does
   that force the player to feel?" (`KkdPxZbUNSw` [0:00:00]-[0:01:39])
8. **Cosmetic-only stat flavor**: "Two swords differ only in name and particle color, not real damage: will
   players still argue which is 'better'?" (`sLXLlJ7FhJU` [0:05:42])

## Verdict line
Does not change the "layer, not pillar" verdict, but thickens the layer: two of eight videos (Extra Credits,
GMTK) are genuinely strong, directly buildable additions (fairness-fudging, pacing pillars, mystery-over-reveal,
long-term scaffolding); the other six restate MDA/GMTK, sit too abstract for a kid spec, or aren't really about
mechanics at all. Ben's instinct that game design is "very important" is reinforced by the two strong videos,
but the batch as a whole — his own picks, several explicitly beginner-level — argues against a third pillar:
even a hand-picked list is 75% recap, off-target, or too abstract, and the strongest quarter still resolves into
worksheets bolted onto character/mechanic design, not a standalone strand.

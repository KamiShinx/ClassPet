# Batch M — two long "cool mechanics" compilations (Game Design Library)

Two videos, same channel, same format: ~100 distinct mechanics catalogued across 66+59 minutes (see per-video notes). This batch is raw material, not analysis — the "strongest ideas" below recurred, stacked with each other, or were unusually clean specs, not ones argued for by the source.

## Strongest ideas across the batch
1. **Modular equipment that changes BOTH combat and traversal** — twice, independently (Eternal Life of Goldman's cane; DMC2's crest). Two unrelated games converging on one shape is a real signal: swap-a-part items are a strong "insane item" spec.
2. **Single-target relentless aggro** (Left 4 Dead 2's specials, echoed by WildGate's shared-doom reactor) — simple, well-tested, and Minecraft already has AI-goal primitives for "always chase this player."
3. **Low-friction companion mob** (Cat Quest 2: mirrors attacks, heals by proximity, you "become" them on death) — the single most complete, buildable spec in either video.
4. **Death/failure feeds forward** — Inscryption's death card and Put Down's stress-spawns-a-threat both turn losing into new content, not just a reset. Good progression material.
5. **A meter that quietly punishes or forgives** — Bag of Dreams' corruption meter and Peggle's secret 10%-per-fail forgiveness are opposite ends of one idea (hidden state shifts difficulty); worth teaching as a pair.
6. **Reuse one trigger, change the payoff by context** — Tinykin's tool-swap and Peggle's green-peg-means-something-different-per-world are the same lesson twice.
7. **Decaying-value pickups punish camping** — Zekesta Z's ticking score and Ball Pit's must-return-before-refire both force movement without a timer or enemy doing it.
8. **Resource-goal reframing under a hard cap** — Overland (fuel, not kills, seat-limited car) is the cleanest "same genre, different objective" idea here.

## Where videos agree or contradict
Both videos independently land on "modular multi-purpose equipment" and "one hidden meter drives a twist" — convergence, not contradiction, which makes these safer bets than one-off ideas. No direct contradictions; the videos don't cover overlapping games. The one soft tension is tonal: video 1 leans horror/tension (Pinnacle Point, Mouthwashing, Kaput Mortem, Don't Look!), while video 2's second half leans precision-physics sports (golf) that mostly fail the "buildable in NeoForge" filter. So the two aren't equally useful per minute — video 1's back half (50:00-66:00) and video 2's front two-thirds (0:00-31:00) are the denser ore.

## What I'd cut
- The golf/mini-golf run in video 2 (31:00-51:49, a third of that video) — precision mouse-swing physics doesn't map onto Minecraft's controls, and it's one genre repeated 8 times, not 8 distinct lessons.
- Bespoke-UI-as-the-mechanic (Heaven Does Not Respond's simulated OS, Marathon's sell filters, Treasure of Rudras' typed spellcrafting) — charming but not a mod mechanic.
- Detective/dialogue mechanics (Viola, Paradise Killer) — same "novel's length" problem CONTEXT.md flags for worldbuilding.
- Turn-based JRPG combat (Grandia's turn meter, Trails in the Sky's mode-switching, Echo Generation 2, Luthier 2's AoE math) — real-time Minecraft can't host these without replacing the whole combat engine.

## Mechanic cards
- **"The push-back potion"** — Vital Shell: an item use heals you to full — what stops players grinding safely instead of engaging? [v1 0:18:25]
- **"The two-piece weapon"** — Goldman's cane / DMC2's crest: swappable parts change both fighting and moving — what's the tradeoff between the best combat combo and the best movement combo? [v1 0:33:10 / v2 0:32:35]
- **"The clingy boss"** — Left 4 Dead 2: a mob that locks onto and chases ONE player no matter what — how does the team react, and what stops "the worst player always dies"? [v1 0:40:09]
- **"The shared self-destruct"** — WildGate: a sabotage-able countdown ends the fight for everyone nearby, regardless of who's winning — how free should triggering it be? [v1 0:50:26]
- **"The buddy you can become"** — Cat Quest 2: a companion who fights with you, heals by proximity, and takes over if you die — what if the companion ALSO dies? [v2 0:26:35]
- **"The fear gauge"** — Put Down: a meter that rises passively and, past a threshold, spawns a real threat, managed only by a limited resource — what's that resource in Minecraft? [v2 0:39:45]
- **"The ticking loot"** — Zekesta Z: a kill-drop worth a lot immediately, decaying fast — how far into danger will a player go to grab it? [v2 0:58:00]
- **"Evacuation math"** — Overland: the goal is a limited resource (fuel/seats), not kills, and you can't save everyone — what's the Minecraft equivalent? [v2 0:46:46]

## Verdict line
This batch **strengthens, but does not overturn**, the "layer, not pillar" verdict. It supplies unusually spec-ready material (the 8 cards above), arguing for widening the existing checklist (MDA / risk-reward dial / boss-role picker / "ask something different" / versatile verbs) by 2-3 items — companion design, modular dual-purpose equipment, decaying/forgiving hidden meters — rather than for a third pillar. The batch's density (100 mechanics, 2 videos) is itself a caveat: raw ore, not a lesson plan, needing a follow-up pass to cut it to the 8-10 cards a class can use, which the section above starts.

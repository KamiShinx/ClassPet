# Batch N synthesis: progression and rewards

8 Design Doc videos (all same channel/format): leveling, New Game Plus, unlockables, collectibles, item hoarding, shops (x2), replayability. Design Doc is essay-style with real taxonomies, not hype content — this batch has the least padding of any I'd expect in this project, and several videos hand us genuinely reusable frameworks rather than just examples.

## Strongest ideas for this class

1. **Criteria + Prize** (Unlockables, R8EyVhHRjqc [0:07:40]): every unlock = what you do (Accumulate or Achieve) + what you get. Best single worksheet skeleton in the batch — spec-able in one page, maps directly onto Minecraft advancements + loot tables, no new engineering needed.
2. **Extrinsic vs. Intrinsic motivation**, independently reached by two videos (Level-Up [0:02:51] and Collectibles [0:01:48]): extrinsic = guaranteed reward, reliable but shallow; intrinsic = the doing is the reward, deeper but harder to guarantee. This is the batch's clearest cross-video agreement and should anchor any "why did I add this mechanic" review question.
3. **Novelty / Creativity / Evolution**, each with a matched failure mode (Replayability, AX_59mS8mr0 [0:02:12]-[0:18:12]): the video explicitly names Minecraft as the textbook Creativity example — confirms that leaning into sandbox/build tools is the cheapest replayability win available to a kid's mod, more than plot or procedural content.
4. **Five causes of item hoarding + matched fixes** (Item Hoarding, HT-Z03YVBPI [0:03:46]-[0:16:20]): the most causally rigorous video in the batch; "charges that recharge at a station" (Estus Flask) is the single best pattern for a custom healing/buff item, and maps cleanly onto NeoForge NBT.
5. **Don't let a bonus system create an accidental "always do this" answer** — this warning recurs three times independently: FFXV's XP hotels (Level-Up [0:15:43]), MGSV's tranquilizer gun (Replayability [0:09:47]), Moonlighter's price-sorted item book (Moonlighter/Recettear [0:08:42]). Worth stating once, generically, as a standing review question for any custom system a kid builds.
6. **The shop complexity ladder** (Shops, B_pfFVXBvic): only its first 2-3 "floors" (basic buy/sell, money caps, split currencies) are realistic for our scope — but those map directly onto villager trading and are genuinely easy wins.
7. **Curse/trade-off items** (Moonlighter/Recettear [0:05:30]): a buff with a built-in drawback is small, fun, and a one-item NBT task — good "quick win" activity.
8. **"Certificate, not coupon"** (Unlockables [0:14:50]): the reward for the HARDEST content should be bragging rights or a fun cosmetic/break-the-rules perk, not a "useful" item you no longer need — good gut-check for boss rewards.

## Where videos agree / contradict
Agreement: three videos independently land on "spread rewards across the whole playthrough, don't front- or back-load them" (Level-Up's multi-track leveling, Unlockables' Accumulate-tier spacing, New Game Plus's God Mode critique). Mild tension, not a real contradiction: New Game Plus praises unlimited "God Mode" power as a valid reward style, while Item Hoarding and Replayability both warn that unlimited power/no-real-cost systems get boring or create dominant strategies fast — worth telling kids both are true depending on whether the power is a one-time treat (fine) or a standing system (needs a check).

## Cut
The Smash Bros unlock-history deep dive, the full NG+ narrative case studies (Nier, Chrono Trigger, Undertale), the item-shop-as-whole-genre analysis (Moonlighter/Recettear is our weakest video — mine it for the curse-item idea only), and the quirky-shop-gimmick examples (haggling minigames, mic-yelling) — the video itself flags these as failures, so they're "what not to do" one-liners, not activities.

## Mechanic cards
- "A healing item that refills only at a station you build — near danger, or safe?" (Item Hoarding, HT-Z03YVBPI [0:08:28])
- "An item heals you but breaks a nearby block when used — worth the trade?" (Moonlighter, lko7osS0AE8 [0:05:30])
- "Beating the hardest boss gives a joke/cosmetic reward, not a stat boost — does that feel earned?" (Unlockables, R8EyVhHRjqc [0:14:50])
- "A trader's stock changes by day — what's only available on one day?" (Shops, B_pfFVXBvic [0:12:58])
- "A trader gives better prices the more you visit — what stops players from gaming it?" (Shops, B_pfFVXBvic [0:13:28])
- "A collectible unlocks nothing — just a hard sound cue and a hard-to-reach spot. Would you still chase it?" (Collectibles, M03bTkQxVUg [0:08:55])
- "A 3-tier enchant path where tier 2 forces one of two choices — what do you give up?" (Level-Up, Ae_zvm0-CQU [0:12:30])
- "A mob gets harder every time you refight it — how many rounds before it's unfair?" (New Game Plus, YK7AuOjX0WE [0:06:35])

## Verdict line
This batch **strengthens** the "layer, not pillar" verdict rather than overturning it: it hands us more concretely spec-able worksheets (Criteria+Prize, the hoarding checklist, extrinsic/intrinsic) than a typical batch, but every one of them works by bending a system Minecraft already has (advancements, trading, enchanting, loot, stack caps) — none requires a new pillar alongside character/worldbuilding design, they're review questions and small item/mechanic specs that sit on top of what a kid is already building.

# Batch O: status effects, elements, poison, crits, luck, risky damage

All 8 videos are Design Doc (same channel/style: dense narration over gameplay-clip montage, zero original diagrams except three text cards — "TRIGGER/BONUS," "FIRST-ORDER OPTIMAL STRATEGY," "Glass Cannons/WITH-WITHOUT"). This is the most internally coherent and most directly Minecraft-relevant batch so far: Minecraft's `MobEffect` system is a genuinely good match for this theme.

## Strongest ideas across the batch

1. **Trigger + Bonus** (fMFkd7edBR0, 0:03:23) — the batch's most reusable framework. Any mechanic can be designed as "what causes it" + "what you get"; fill-in-able as a two-column worksheet in 15 min.
2. **First-Order Optimal Strategy** (NmwjgvOYx5Q 0:02:54, QS9CYuUOmU4 0:02:55) — shared throughline: if the best move never changes, the game gets boring; every system in this batch exists to break this. Teach once, explicitly.
3. **Poison as 5 buildable modes** (a44AyNXjYXo) — investment DoT, capability cap, area hazard, build-around resource, pure aesthetic. Cleanest worksheet source, since Poison is already a real Minecraft effect.
4. **Buildup-meter status** (a44AyNXjYXo 0:04:32, ThDVGP4UB30 0:13:34) — status as a meter that fills, with rising resistance after each cure, not a binary hit/miss. Appears in two videos independently; needs persistent per-entity data, so it's an advanced tier.
5. **Reskin it** (gEPeGx6IVUQ 0:02:34) — rename + one flavor twist on an existing effect is legitimate, low-effort design; good first exercise.
6. **One system, one status** (gEPeGx6IVUQ 0:09:27, Glohm) — the best statuses warp ONE system the game already has, not a generic buff/debuff. Maps directly to "pick one Minecraft system and warp it."
7. **Glass cannon, don't-do-it-by-accident pitfall** (NmwjgvOYx5Q 0:06:05, Danger Mario) — useful check question for any kid mechanic: could a player get the upside with none of the downside?
8. **Fire Emblem's True Hit system** (4cmGSMDOrfc 0:09:18) — the most rigorously explained mechanic in the batch; a natural bridge to percentage/probability understanding via loot-drop tuning.
9. **A little control changes how luck feels** (4cmGSMDOrfc 0:14:05, Mario Party) — a re-roll item improves feel far more than the underlying math changes.

## Where videos agree or contradict

- **Strong agreement**: ThDVGP4UB30, gEPeGx6IVUQ, bS808xaLaFo (status-effect trilogy) and a44AyNXjYXo (poison) all converge on the same lesson: a good status ties into ONE system the game already has. The batch's most load-bearing, cross-confirmed idea.
- **Shared vocabulary, not a new insight**: NmwjgvOYx5Q and QS9CYuUOmU4 both use "First-Order Optimal Strategy / Strategic Dominance" — cite once for the class, not as two separate findings.
- **Self-aware overreach**: bS808xaLaFo stretches "status effect" very far (word-puzzle curses, narrative-branch personality stats) and admits it ("stay with me here," 0:13:48) — flag to kids rather than treat as settled.
- **No factual contradictions found** between any two videos — an unusually coherent batch because it's one channel's connected mini-series that cites itself throughout.

## Ideas to cut

- Cassette Beasts' 14-type chart and Pokemon's cycles-within-cycles (gEPeGx6IVUQ, QS9CYuUOmU4) — whole combat systems, not one-page specs.
- Disco Elysium's stat-branching narrative and Cursed Words' curse-as-genre-transformer (bS808xaLaFo) — need dialogue trees / a bespoke puzzle engine, well outside NeoForge's easy bucket.
- True narrative permadeath (Fuga, NmwjgvOYx5Q) — too heavy for 11-13 and fights Minecraft's respawn design; reframe as losing an item/pet, never "dies forever."
- Precise skill-timing systems (Expedition 33 parries, Active Reload, Super Guard) — harder for Gemini than the brief's easy bucket; advanced/optional only.

## Mechanic cards

1. **Capability-cap poison**: "A poison that doesn't hurt you, but halves your swing speed and mining speed until it wears off." (a44AyNXjYXo, 0:08:48, Symphony of the Night)
2. **Buildup-meter status**: "A status that only lands after enough hits fill an invisible meter, and gets harder to reapply each time — how do you show progress without a number?" (a44AyNXjYXo 0:04:32 / ThDVGP4UB30 0:13:34, Monster Hunter)
3. **One-system status**: "Pick one thing your mod already has and design a status that warps just that one thing." (gEPeGx6IVUQ, 0:09:27, Glohm)
4. **Glass cannon with a catch**: "An item that doubles damage dealt and taken — what stops a player from buying back the downside for free?" (NmwjgvOYx5Q, 0:05:03 & 0:06:05)
5. **Scarce power item**: "One weapon, one shot, instantly clears any threat — where do you place it, and what happens if a player wastes it early?" (NmwjgvOYx5Q, 0:09:24, Requiem Magnum)
6. **Trigger + Bonus crit**: "Pick how your crit is caused (random / sneak attack / timed swing) and what it gives you (damage / a status / a downside too)." (fMFkd7edBR0, 0:03:23-0:16:55)
7. **Negative crit twist**: "A weapon that sometimes crits weak instead of strong, but has high base damage — when would a player equip it?" (fMFkd7edBR0, 0:15:46, Monster Hunter Affinity)
8. **A little luck control**: "A charm that lets the player re-roll one bad drop per day — how much control is enough to feel fair without killing tension?" (4cmGSMDOrfc, 0:14:05)

## Verdict line

**Strengthens** the "layer, not pillar" verdict, and sharpens it: this batch shows game-design content adds value only when scoped to a single system Minecraft already has (poison, crits, drop luck, glass-cannon trades). Every buildable idea here is a checklist item layered onto an existing mechanic, not a new pillar — confirming game design belongs as a worksheet layer on character/item design. Worth adding one question to the existing checklist (MDA, risk/reward dial, boss-role picker, "does this mob ask something different," versatile verbs): "which ONE existing system does this warp?" — the idea every video in this batch converges on independently.

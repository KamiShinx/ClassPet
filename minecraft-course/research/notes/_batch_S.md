# Batch S: companions, rivals, narrative choice, style, cozy, stealing (8 Design Doc videos)

## Overview
All 8 videos are one channel (Design Doc), one format (narrated essay over gameplay b-roll, no diagrams). This batch sits at the "character meets mechanics" seam, but splits hard: companions (x2), rivals, and stealing give real, buildable MOB mechanics. Party chemistry, narrative choice, and cozy are mostly writing/production craft; style is an aesthetics argument. Three of eight are effectively worldbuilding/character-design content mis-filed under "game mechanics."

## The 5-8 strongest ideas across the batch
1. **Thief mob, full spec** (FevGJOU54Xo [3:02]-[9:58]): steal-and-flee mob, chaseable, detection/consequence layered (sneak to avoid, aggro on failure). Best match to this batch's own "thief mob" prompt — genuinely buildable in NeoForge.
2. **Rival mob that mirrors and escalates** (58FL3yIkJSs [2:15]/[4:54]): copies the player's current weapon/enchant, gets stronger each defeat, recurs over time. This is really "boss + persistence" — folds into the existing boss-role-picker checklist rather than standing alone.
3. **"One job" companion spec** (tIze-mre0ss [1:48], pfNzYJ0S8Oo [7:03]): before building a tamed companion, name its ONE utility (buff/distract/detect) and accept it never out-damages the player. Two videos converge on the same worksheet — teach once.
4. **A weak mechanic needs real upside or nobody uses it** (FevGJOU54Xo [11:06]-[13:38], Dragon Quest vs. FF9 steal rates): a clean, quotable negative-design rule, portable beyond stealing to any risk/reward mechanic.
5. **Living-UI companion** (pfNzYJ0S8Oo [6:30]): a companion whose look reflects a stat (health/buff) already has a working Minecraft precedent (wolf tail/whimper) — cheap to point kids at and extend.
6. **Blockiness is a feature, not an apology** (I8FmIya47lc [9:58], Adam Vian quote): low-detail models leave room for imagination — useful for a kid whose Blockbench model looks "too simple." Worldbuilding-adjacent, not a mechanic.
7. **Low-consequence design via existing game rules** (g4yT-uAUcSA [3:57]/[5:36]): a "cozy" biome/dimension (no fall damage, no hostiles, easy respawn) is a scoped, real NeoForge task.
8. **Reuse one structure, swap its state on a flag** (Gdt5zCdXoSc [6:23], Tactics Ogre): the only "narrative choice on a budget" trick that survives — gate a structure's loot/mob spawns on an advancement instead of building two structures. Stretch idea.

## Where videos agree or contradict
- The two companion videos agree closely: scope the companion to one job, keep failure low-cost, match promised role to actual capability. No contradiction — teach as one lesson.
- The rival video is functionally an extension of the boss-design material (batches P1/P2), not a new category; its "persistence" factor is the addition worth folding into the existing checklist.
- The narrative-choice video is the batch's clearest internal counter-argument to "add more character-driven content": even AAA studios can't afford real branching cheaply, which argues against dialogue trees or multi-ending stories here — reinforcing the "layer, not pillar" verdict rather than weakening it.
- Style and cozy don't contradict anything but aren't mechanics videos — likely routed here for being adjacent to "character," but their content belongs on the worldbuilding/character-design side.

## Ideas to cut
- Party chemistry (W3mwVQZFyfE): almost nothing survives — assumes a large, scripted, multi-hour cast. Cut; at most a flavor-text mention that mobs can share a backstory.
- Narrative choice's branching/meter/ending systems (everything except the map-reuse trick): cut, no dialogue-tree or save-branch system exists in this stack.
- Style's auteur-scale material (Suda51 section): cut, far too large a concept for one kid in 20 weeks; keep only the Adam Vian quote as a talking point.
- Cozy's curatorial padding (roughly 40% of runtime recommending A Short Hike): cut, not teachable content.

## Mechanic cards
1. **"A tamed companion mob that gives a buff or utility, never damage — what's its one job, and what happens on the turns it doesn't help?"** — tIze-mre0ss [1:48], pfNzYJ0S8Oo [7:03].
2. **"A rival mob that copies your current weapon/enchantments, gets stronger each time you beat it, and comes back days later — how many times before it's 'done'?"** — 58FL3yIkJSs [2:15]/[4:54].
3. **"A mob that steals an item from your hotbar and runs — how do you get it back, and what stops you one-shotting it before it steals?"** — FevGJOU54Xo [3:02].
4. **"A pickpocket interaction with a real chance of failure — what happens when you fail, and is the reward worth the risk?"** — FevGJOU54Xo [6:18]/[11:06]-[13:38].
5. **"A companion mob whose appearance changes to show you something (mood, buff, health) with no UI element — what's it telling you?"** — pfNzYJ0S8Oo [6:30].
6. **"A peaceful biome or mini-dimension with no fail-state — no fall damage, no hostiles, easy respawn. What's still fun to do there?"** — g4yT-uAUcSA [3:57]/[5:36].
7. **"Reuse one structure for two outcomes — swap its loot table and mob spawns based on something the player already did."** (stretch) — Gdt5zCdXoSc [6:23].

## Verdict line
This batch does not overturn "layer, not pillar" — it sharpens it. The two videos yielding real mechanics (rival, thief) are existing checklist items in new clothes: rival = boss + persistence, thief = risk/reward interaction + mob AI. Neither needs a new pillar. Narrative choice actively argues against adding a story-branching pillar; party chemistry and most of style confirm "character" content stays on the worldbuilding side rather than generating new code. Net effect: two or three new mechanic-card prompts added to the existing checklist layer, no case for a third pillar.

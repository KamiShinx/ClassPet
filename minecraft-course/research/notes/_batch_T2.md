# Batch T2: Genre-specific case studies - synthesis

Nine Design Doc videos: roguelikes (x2), kart racers (x3), Sonic gameplay variety, on-rails games, speedrun-friendly design, rhythm game UX. As the batch brief predicted, this is a low-relevance batch: eight of nine videos analyze genre mechanics (racing physics, rail-shooter pacing, rhythm timing, speedrun infrastructure) with no Minecraft equivalent. One video (Sonic & Gameplay Variety) is a genuine standout that speaks directly to Ben's stated worry.

## The strongest ideas across the batch

1. **"One core verb - does this extend it or dilute it?" - "Sonic & Gameplay Variety" [qGM-dPerv1A].** The best-fitting idea in the batch, matching the batch theme's own hint. Sonic 3 & Knuckles succeeds because Tails/Knuckles are variations under one shared ruleset; Sonic Adventure fails by recycling one level for five playstyles it wasn't built for; Sonic Colors succeeds again because its Wisp powers all share one trait (movement) and stay optional; Sonic Lost World fails hard by piling on eight-plus disconnected minigame styles with no identifiable core left. A scope-control checklist for kids' mod specs.

2. **"Built for it, or bolted on?" - same video [0:04:56].** When a kid reuses one build for a second mechanic, check whether the space was actually designed for it or just recycled. Pushback against over-scoping a single project.

3. **"Apply a mechanic all the way, or it reads as tacked on" - "Rhythm Game UX" [nRJ4iVjIMAc, 0:10:17-0:12:23].** Hi-Fi Rush ties its rhythm mechanic to world, attacks, AND enemies - it feels essential. No Straight Roads only applies it to enemies, not the player, and feels disconnected. A distinct angle from idea 1: once you add a mechanic, commit it everywhere it logically belongs rather than half-applying it to the flashiest system.

4. **"Teach it, repeat it, test it" - "Evolution of Mario Kart Tracks" [dOeKp-FzlO8, 0:24:07].** A track (Mushroom Gorge) introduces a mechanic simply, repeats it harder, then offers a branching choice requiring mastery. Reusable three-beat structure for a custom dungeon room, though it repackages familiar scaffolding rather than adding new insight.

5. **"Bolt an extra rule onto an existing structure" - "Roguelikes Are Getting Weird" [_xYSQe9oq6c, 0:08:44] / "Evolution of Roguelike Design" [uM588ci-sMQ, 0:05:06].** A roguelike can be one scarcity/randomness rule layered on a system that already exists (BAZR bolts a card-deck resource limit onto vanilla Mario 64). The companion video's Berlin-Interpretation wheel (8 elements, swap one, note the compensation) is a clean spec-writing frame, but a real payoff (a regenerating dungeon with permadeath-lite) is a multi-session build, not a week's homework.

6. **"Prove it, don't tell it" - "Speedrun Friendly" [ixmOKhM_AlI, 0:05:13].** Teach a mechanic via a safe immediate test (Celeste's double-jump platform) instead of a sign to read. Generic instructional design, not new relative to what a good teacher already knows.

## Where videos agree or contradict

One real throughline: three videos (Sonic variety, rhythm UX, Mario Kart tracks) independently converge on "commit to a mechanic across the whole system, or it dilutes/feels tacked on" - Sonic Lost World's grab-bag of minigames, No Straight Roads' half-applied rhythm, and Mushroom Gorge's disciplined single-mechanic teaching are the same lesson from three unrelated genres. Worth telling Ben: not one YouTuber's opinion, three analyses land on it independently. No real contradictions otherwise - the racing, rail-shooter, and speedrun videos don't engage with this question at all.

## Ideas to cut

- Genre taxonomy/trivia: Berlin Interpretation history, roguelike-vs-roguelite debates, decade-by-decade Mario Kart track catalogs, rail-shooter genre history.
- Kart item-balancing ("comeback mechanics"), kart physics, course theming - no Minecraft equivalent; competitive lap racing isn't in scope.
- Speedrun infrastructure (leaderboards, timers, rank gates) - live-service/meta-game features, out of scope for a solo-mod class.
- Rhythm-specific engineering (lag calibration, note-highway sight-reading) - no rhythm gameplay exists in Minecraft.
- On-rails spectacle-density lessons - the only Minecraft hook (a minecart boss-ride) is a narrow one-off.

## Mechanic cards

- **"Sonic Colors, not Sonic Lost World"**: name the ONE core thing your mod is about; does a new idea extend it or bolt on a separate mini-game? [qGM-dPerv1A, 0:08:14 vs 0:10:19]
- **"Built for it or bolted on?"**: reusing a structure for a second mechanic - does the space actually fit it? [qGM-dPerv1A, 0:04:56]
- **"All the way or not at all"**: if a mechanic should touch combat AND movement AND visuals, does it touch all of them? [nRJ4iVjIMAc, 0:10:17]
- **"Teach it, repeat it, test it"**: show a mechanic safely once, repeat it harder, then force a choice that needs it. [dOeKp-FzlO8, 0:24:07]
- **"One extra rule on an old structure"**: pick a Minecraft system and add one scarcity/randomness rule - e.g. "one random ore type is disabled each visit." [_xYSQe9oq6c, 0:08:44]
- **"Prove it, don't tell it"**: teach via a safe first try instead of a sign to read. [ixmOKhM_AlI, 0:05:13]

## Verdict line

**Strengthens the "layer, not pillar" verdict, and adds one genuinely new checklist item.** This batch produces almost no new Minecraft mechanic content the way the earlier game-design batch did (MDA, risk/reward, boss roles) - eight of nine videos are genre case studies with no Minecraft surface area, exactly as the batch theme predicted. But the Sonic video's scope-control framing ("one core verb - extend it or dilute it?") is a distinct, useful addition: a discipline for deciding what NOT to add, which the existing checklists (MDA, risk/reward, boss roles, versatile verbs) don't directly cover. Recommend it as a sixth short checklist alongside the existing five, still a 15-20 minute worksheet layered onto character design -> vibecoding, not a new pillar.

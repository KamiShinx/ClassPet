# The 7 Deadly Sins of Worldbuilding (Large Lads Studios, 20.8 min)

**What it is:** A checklist-style video mapping the 7 Catholic deadly sins onto 7 common worldbuilding mistakes: Pride = lore dump, Greed = monetizing before building anything, Wrath = edgelord/needless-darkness escalation, Envy = cloning other IPs, Lust = rule-of-cool without consequences, Gluttony = world builder's disease, Sloth = never finishing.
**Substance:** substantive at the idea level (each sin gets a clear definition, example, and fix) but heavily padded with reaction-meme cutaways, jokes, and repeated Patreon/Discord/like-button plugs — call it mixed.

## Ideas, in the video's order
- [0:01:07] Sin 1, Pride — the lore dump: explaining a world in the order you invented it (creation myth -> pantheon -> dynasties) bores audiences; "people don't become interested in information because they're interested in the world," not the reverse. Fix: ask "does the audience need to know this right now?" and hold it back if not.
- [0:03:52] Sin 2, Greed — wanting to monetize a world (Patreon, merch, publishing) before it has an audience or finished work; fix is audience-and-quality first, money later. This is about online creator-community behavior, not worldbuilding craft itself.
- [0:06:33] Sin 3, Wrath — "edgelord's disease": escalating darkness/violence as a competition rather than a storytelling tool; constant horror stops landing, and it often makes settings internally illogical (societies that "should" have collapsed keep functioning).
- [0:09:48] Sin 4, Envy — cloning: copying rather than being inspired; his test (credited to Brandon Sanderson) is asking how common a trope is and how big/specific its source is (dragon riding = fine; "eat metal for magic" = too close to Sanderson's Allomancy). Also covers maps that are recognizably real countries or other IP's maps traced.
- [0:11:58] Sin 5, Lust — "rule of cool" taken too far: cool ideas (dragons, floating castles) need consequences/depth or they create shallow "holes"; the fix isn't fewer cool ideas but following through on how each one would actually change things (a floating city needs a real answer for trade/logistics).
- [0:15:16] Sin 6, Gluttony — "world builder's disease": the compulsive urge to keep building details that never get used while the actual project (novel/campaign/game) stalls; named as the most dangerous sin because it feels productive.
- [0:17:27] Sin 7, Sloth — never finishing: starting new projects whenever the current one gets hard, because early-stage ideas are always exciting and unflawed; "the most successful creators aren't the ones with the most ideas... they picked one and stuck with it."
- [0:19:36] Closing caveat: every sin is fine in small doses (a little pride/lust/gluttony can produce something good); the danger is only when one habit takes over completely.

## What the frames add
Nothing. Across all 6 contact sheets the visuals are almost entirely fast-cut reaction memes and anime/movie/TikTok clips illustrating jokes (Spongebob writing memes, an "I'm greed" anime clip, Kermit walking away, a Dolce & Gabbana ad, Simpsons clips, Skeletor) with zero diagrams, checklists, or on-screen text summaries of the sins themselves. The video is entirely audio-carried; the frames confirm the "heavily padded" read on substance.

## For our class (11-13, Minecraft mod, Gemini writes the code)
### Becomes something kids do
- **"3 sins pre-flight checklist"** (5 min, reusable every week): before building, kids check the 3 sins that actually apply — Pride ("am I about to write more lore than I'm about to build?"), Lust ("does my cool item/mob change anything else in the game?"), Gluttony ("have I spent this whole session planning instead of opening Antigravity?"). Concrete and reusable weekly.
- **"Cool-thing consequence" worksheet**: for any new item/mob/structure, one required follow-up question — "name one other thing in the world that is now different because this exists" (a shop price, a mob behavior, a block that only appears nearby) — direct lift from the Lust sin's fix.
- **"Finish it" rule as course policy**, not a kid worksheet: since Sloth (always starting a shinier new idea) is a realistic risk across 20 weekly sessions with 8 kids, this should be teacher-enforced ("you build on last week's world, you don't restart") rather than self-policed.

### Survives the move to Minecraft?
Pride, Lust, and Gluttony transfer directly and well — they're about the balance between planning/lore and actually building, exactly the tension the course structure will hit (a "platform" for writing lore could let kids lore-dump for weeks and never open Blockbench or Antigravity). Envy/cloning transfers as "don't just re-texture a creeper," though the video's own examples (map-tracing, prose-magic-system plagiarism) are about published fiction, not mods.

### Doesn't transfer
Greed is entirely about online creator-economy behavior (Patreon, merch, publishers) — an 8-kid classroom project has no audience or monetization angle, so this sin should be cut outright, not adapted. Wrath/edgelord is a weaker fit too: it's specifically about competitive dark/violent content escalation in online fantasy communities; 11-13-year-olds are unlikely to hit this, though it could be loosely reframed as a game-balance idea ("if every mob is a boss-tier threat, none of them feel special") if the teacher wants to keep it.

## Honest caveats
Per the brief's specific question: the checklist framing (7 short, memorable, example-backed rules) is genuinely well-suited to "tooltip" reuse, but 2 of the 7 sins are squarely about published-fiction/online-creator-economy problems and do not apply to a kid's Minecraft mod — don't force all 7 into the curriculum. The video's own examples (Warhammer 40K, Berserk, Sanderson's Cosmere, Reddit map critiques) assume an audience already fluent in fantasy fandom, which 11-13-year-olds may not have; the teacher would need simpler substitute examples.

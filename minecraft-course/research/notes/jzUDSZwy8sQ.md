# Every Cool Game Mechanic I Covered in July 2026 (Game Design Library, 59 min)

**What it is:** Same format as the channel's other compilation (batch M's other video): one segment per game, ~40 games, screen-recording only, Whisper-transcribed (more reliable text than the other video's auto-captions). Several games recur for multiple, separately-timestamped mechanics (How Many Dudes x6, Denshi Attack x5, Ballgame/Golf games form a whole back half of the video — this appears to be a themed "golf games" stretch from roughly 31:00 to the end).

**Substance:** substantive, same as the other batch-M video — dense, specific, mostly free of padding. The back third (31:00-51:49) is noticeably narrower: it's 8 different golf/mini-golf games in a row, which reads as a themed sub-compilation rather than "every cool mechanic," worth noting if the teacher wants variety over volume.

## Catalogue: every distinct mechanic, in order

Format: `[timestamp] Game — what it does. MC: yes/no/maybe (why)`

- [0:00:24] **Death's Peck** — getting cut in half unlocks a double jump; healing mushrooms are something to actively AVOID because they remove your double jump. Inverts "avoid damage." MC: yes — strong one: a self-damage/split mechanic that unlocks a new movement ability, with healing framed as a cost not a benefit, is a genuinely fresh item/status-effect spec.
- [0:02:20] **Blood Dungeon** — climb any surface with zero stamina/cooldown penalty; enemies are sorted by size and wall-affinity (stick to walls / phase through / shoot through), turning movement itself into the puzzle. MC: yes (a mob-type roster where each type interacts differently with terrain — flies through walls, sticks to walls, etc — is a good buildable mob-variety spec).
- [0:04:03] **How Many Dudes** — before each fight, choose easy (safer, less money) vs hard (riskier, more money), read against a visible timeline showing the next shop/boss. MC: yes (a simple risk/reward branch — easier path less loot, harder path more loot, shown on a visible countdown to the next boss — is a clean structure spec, e.g. for a dungeon's route choice).
- [0:05:19] **Trails in the Sky** — freely switch between real-time (to herd/position enemies for AoE) and turn-based combat; positioning enemies together in real-time sets up a better turn-based AoE hit. MC: maybe (full turn-based combat is a big system; the underlying idea — group enemies together before triggering AoE damage — could inspire a simpler "combo" mob-clustering status effect).
- [0:06:58] **Grandia** — a "COM/ACT" turn meter desyncs over the fight: units select at COM, execute at ACT, and a fast unit can interrupt/cancel a slow unit's move before it fires. MC: no (needs a full custom turn-based combat engine; not how Minecraft fights work).
- [0:08:25] **Luthier 2** — AoE attacks let the player CHOOSE how many enemies to hit; each extra target roughly halves the damage, shown via a visible attack-range bar. MC: maybe (a weapon with a toggle between "single strong hit" and "wide weak hit" is a reasonable item-ability spec, without the full JRPG targeting UI).
- [0:10:22] **Treasure of Rudras** — literally type letters to craft a custom spell; the game parses substrings for element/verb/cost from a lookup table, with no hard restriction beyond mana cost. MC: no (needs real text-parsing input and a large lookup table — too much scope and UI for one page, though conceptually charming).
- [0:11:49] **Mini Mini Golf Golf** — watching FMV clips on an in-fiction second screen tells you HOW (not just "score well") to complete a course to progress the story. MC: no (needs FMV/video assets and a narrative layer; out of scope).
- [0:13:13] **Eats** — placing objects changes a carried creature's AI mood (fear, anger) via thrown items; that mood carries over into the NEXT level, becoming part of its puzzle. MC: yes (a mob whose behavior is set by an item you throw at it, and whose behavior persists across the player's next interaction with it, is a fun and buildable AI-state spec).
- [0:14:30] **Grit Shot** — passive perks resize scoring zones on a shot-timing QTE; stickers attached to specific balls must ALSO be hit within the same short window to trigger their bonus, synergizing two separate systems. MC: no (needs a bespoke QTE/minigame UI Minecraft doesn't have).
- [0:16:05] **Switch Track** — one input flips a track-switch actor between two positions; difficulty scales only by adding more tracks/cart types, no new verbs. MC: maybe ("more of the same simple lever, more complexity from combinations" is a good design LESSON to teach, even if the literal minigame doesn't map).
- [0:17:14] **Slayblade** — combat is pure velocity: whoever has higher closing speed when two moving objects collide wins the clash and the other takes damage. MC: yes (a mob or minigame where two moving things colliding compares speed/momentum to decide the winner is a fresh, buildable combat-adjacent idea, e.g. a battering-ram item).
- [0:20:42] **Denshi Attack (drift/jump)** — drift is a hold-then-release-in-a-window timing challenge, auto-started by the game as a "reminder" at corners; jump is charge-then-release with barrel-roll steering. MC: maybe (a charge-and-release timing mechanic — e.g. a charged attack with a "perfect" release window — is buildable, without the literal on-rails vehicle).
- [0:21:11] **Inching Along** — 5-input caterpillar locomotion (2 feet grips, scrunch, 2 lean inputs), deliberately kept at/under ~6 inputs (subitizing principle) so it stays learnable; letting go of everything flops you into a checkpoint as relief. MC: no (needs custom soft-body/procedural-animation movement; too complex for a first mod), but the "cap complexity to ~6 things a player must track" design LESSON is worth teaching directly.
- [0:22:20] **Warhammer 40K: Boltgun 2** — allies just die if you shoot them, no invulnerability and no penalty flag — a silent, un-punished extra difficulty lever for players who want it. MC: yes (friendly-fire-enabled companions with no forced penalty is a one-line toggle a kid could spec for a companion mob).
- [0:23:15] **Ballgame** — shows a dotted trajectory preview, lets you add spin (changes both curve and landing bounce), and lets you slow-walk the ball short distances with the stick. MC: maybe (a trajectory-preview line for thrown items, like a trident or snowball, is a nice buildable UI touch if Gemini can do simple render overlays).
- [0:24:39] **Echo Generation 2** — enemies never get an independent turn; they only counter right after your attack (partial-block QTE) or ambush at a new wave's start, so the player must track an unstated turn order by observation. MC: no (bespoke turn-based combat UI, not a fit).
- [0:26:35] **Cat Quest 2** — AI companion mirrors your attacks from another angle (rarely draws aggro), heals by proximity-standing (no separate revive button, you can still fight while healing them), and you seamlessly swap control to them if you die. MC: yes — strong one: a friendly mob that fights alongside you, whom you can revive just by standing near, and who you can "become" if you die, is a great buildable companion-mob spec.
- [0:28:38] **Denshi Attack (grading)** — separately grades trick-score and time, rewarding two genuinely different playstyles per level rather than one dominant strategy. MC: yes (giving a challenge/structure TWO separate scoreboards that reward different approaches — e.g. "fastest" vs "most style points" — is a clean advancement-system spec).
- [0:30:21] **Ballgame (incentives)** — optional in-level pickups (money, extra-stroke gems) compete against the core "fewest shots" goal, creating a real sacrifice-now-for-later-benefit decision. MC: maybe (optional risk pickups inside a timed/limited-attempt challenge is a fine structure, though "strokes" doesn't map directly to Minecraft).
- [0:31:41] **Kinda Hard Golf** — vertical (not horizontal) mini-golf platforming with Wordle-style daily challenges; must stop dead ON a checkpoint flag (not just near it) to bank it. MC: no (browser mini-golf platformer genre, not a Minecraft-shaped mechanic).
- [0:32:35] **Devil May Cry 2** — a modular 3-part "crest" system builds a custom power-up (transformation) that has BOTH combat and traversal effects (e.g. one part grants flight). MC: yes — strong one, same shape as Eternal Life of Goldman's cane in the other video: modular equipment where swapping parts changes both fighting AND movement is a great spec for an "insane" custom item.
- [0:33:21] **Denshi Attack (vehicle segments)** — periodic non-rail vehicle interludes (boat, ferris wheel) grant full lateral freedom to break up the on-rails structure and vary the "spectacle." MC: no (needs a full separate vehicle-control system; too much scope).
- [0:34:37] **How Many Dudes (consumables)** — mid-fight consumables (revive, spell, teleport) shift a normally-idle game into active play once the run gets hard enough to need them. MC: yes (giving the player limited "panic button" items usable mid-encounter — revive, teleport-out, area heal — is a clean, buildable advancement/inventory spec).
- [0:35:37] **Golf Peaks** — numbered shot-type cards (chip/putt, distance) must be matched against terrain hazards (hills roll you back if you don't clear them, sand needs a chip shot, walls bounce you) — a card-hand-as-resource puzzle. MC: no (card-hand puzzle UI, not a fit for a real-time Minecraft mod).
- [0:38:00] **Peggle** — the green peg's power-up is completely different per world (better aim-line, ball-clone, respawning "spooky ball," wider hole) — one peg TYPE, constantly refreshed meaning. MC: yes (reusing one item/block but changing its effect by biome/world-area is a nice "same trigger, different payoff by context" spec — e.g. a shrine that does something different per biome).
- [0:39:45] **Put Down** — a rising stress meter triggers scripted horror events that directly interfere with the CORE shot mechanic (a hallucinated attacker you must shoot instead of the ball); a limited "cigarette" resource lowers stress over time. MC: yes (a status-effect meter that rises passively and, past a threshold, spawns a real hazard/mob you must deal with, manageable by a limited-use item, is a very buildable tension mechanic — e.g. a "fear" status effect that spawns Endermen-like threats).
- [0:41:49] **Super Battle Golf** — race-to-the-hole (not fewest strokes) golf where every power-up trades movement/control against speed (coffee = pure speed, golf cart = fast but hard to steer, spring jump = big move but no air control, mines/guns = must stop moving to use). MC: yes (a set of items that are all "fast but risky" in different ways, for a race or PvP mode, is a strong item-family spec kids could split up and each design one).
- [0:44:00] **Denshi Attack (dares/collectibles)** — fully optional side-objectives in specific spots reward replayability without gating the main path. MC: yes — simple and broadly useful: optional hidden objectives (advancements) in specific world locations that reward but don't gate progress is a very buildable structure/advancement spec.
- [0:45:19] **How Many Dudes (UI)** — a post-fight breakdown (best/worst unit, support vs combat contribution) plus live healing-bar feedback communicates a deep sim underneath a jokey idle-game skin. MC: no (a full stats/analytics screen is UI scope beyond a first mod, though "show the player a summary of what happened" is a good general lesson).
- [0:46:46] **Overland** — turn-based tactics where the real objective is finding fuel (not killing enemies), and your car has a literal seat limit forcing you to leave rescued units behind. MC: yes — strong one: reframing a "kill everything" structure so survival is actually about gathering a limited resource under a hard capacity cap (forcing hard choices about who/what you can bring) is a great structure to spec for a Minecraft "escape" or "evacuation" build challenge.
- [0:48:19] **Normal Golf Game** — physics-precision swing: WHERE the mouse-swing contacts the ball controls spin/power together, and holding 4 separate keys to set curve/angle (which drift if you stop adjusting them) makes "just hitting the ball" the whole challenge. MC: no (needs precise physics-based aiming input scheme; not a natural Minecraft fit).
- [0:50:45] **Peggle (secret difficulty)** — orange-peg count quietly drops ~10% per failed retry — an undisclosed dynamic-difficulty/forgiveness system. MC: maybe (a boss whose required "hits" or HP threshold silently drops a little each time the player fails is a neat hidden-difficulty idea, good as a discussion point even if not built).
- [0:51:49] **Binary Golf** — collect red squares in any order on open courses; whichever one you collect LAST becomes the hole, letting the player choose their own finish point. MC: yes (a structure where players collect several trigger points in any order, and the LAST one they touch becomes the "goal," is a fresh and buildable puzzle-block spec).
- [0:52:58] **Spanky "Bat-a-Swing"** — rhythm-platforming ties different inputs (not just "hit on beat") to different actions, and mistimed inputs still land (just less effectively) so failure never fully stops your flow. MC: maybe ("forgive the player, let a mistimed action still do something" is a good general combat-feel lesson; literal rhythm-input systems are hard to build well).
- [0:55:02] **Denshi Attack (grinding)** — grinding shares the same left/right stick input as steering, so transitioning from a grind into a jump onto another track requires pre-committing your stick direction before leaving the rail. MC: no (vehicle/rail movement system, not applicable).
- [0:56:12] **Beyond Citadel** — deliberately slow, multi-input reload (spam R to chamber one round, X to move bullets, C to cock) turns reloading into an active resource-tracking decision, subverting typical boomer-shooter reload norms. MC: maybe (a weapon with a genuinely slow, multi-step reload as a balancing cost for high power is a fine item-balance spec, though the literal multi-input scheme is a lot of UI).
- [0:58:00] **Zekesta Z** — kill-drops are worth 10x their number and immediately start ticking down to 1, forcing players to move toward the kill (not camp a safe corner) to cash in the full value. MC: yes (a decaying pickup — worth more immediately after a kill, worth less the longer you wait to grab it — is a simple, buildable anti-camping incentive for loot drops).

## For our class (11-13, Minecraft mod, Gemini writes the code)
### Becomes something kids do
- **"Companion design" worksheet (30 min)**: build on Cat Quest 2's companion (mirrors your attacks, heals by proximity, you "become" them on death) — kids spec a friendly mob with those three rules for their own world's lore character.
- **"Two scoreboards" worksheet (20 min)**: apply Denshi Attack's trick-score/time-score split — kids pick a structure or challenge in their mod and design two different ways to "win" it that reward different playstyles.
- **"Limited panic button" worksheet (20 min)**: How Many Dudes' consumables + Super Battle Golf's power-ups — kids design 2-3 items that are each fast/strong but risky in a different way, meant to be used once per fight.
- **"Escape under a cap" activity (40 min, could be a small build challenge)**: Overland's fuel-not-kills reframe — design a structure/event where the goal is gathering a limited resource under a hard capacity limit, forcing the player to choose what/who to leave behind.

### Survives the move to Minecraft?
The strongest carryovers here are status-effect-shaped: a rising "stress" meter that spawns real threats (Put Down), a decaying-value pickup (Zekesta Z), friendly-fire-without-penalty toggles (Boltgun 2), and modular equipment that changes both combat and movement (DMC2's crest, echoing the other video's cane). These all reduce to NBT/status-effect logic NeoForge handles natively. The golf-genre mechanics (half this video) mostly don't survive — they depend on precise mouse-based physics aiming and course/hole structures that don't exist in Minecraft's block-and-mob vocabulary; at best they're worth ONE lesson ("same trigger, different payoff by context," from Peggle's reused green peg) rather than a build.

### Doesn't transfer
The whole golf/mini-golf run (Golf Peaks, Kinda Hard Golf, Normal Golf Game, Binary Golf partially, Super Battle Golf partially) needs precision physics-aiming input that Minecraft's control scheme doesn't support well — these are worth showing kids as inspiration-only, not as build targets. Treasure of Rudras' typed-spellcrafting and Trails in the Sky/Grandia/Echo Generation 2's turn-based combat systems all need either real text parsing or a full alternate combat engine — too much infrastructure for a 65-min/week, 20-week course.

### Verdict line
This batch **does not weaken** the "layer, not pillar" verdict from the earlier game-design batch, but it does **add real weight to the checklist**: several mechanics here (Cat Quest 2's companion rules, Left-4-Dead-style single-target aggro from the sister video, the risk/reward "harder fight = more loot" branch) are concrete enough to become their OWN worksheet lines alongside MDA/risk-reward-dial/boss-role-picker, not just examples under them. If anything, this batch argues for widening that checklist by 2-3 items (companion design, decaying-value pickups, modular dual-purpose equipment) rather than for a third pillar.

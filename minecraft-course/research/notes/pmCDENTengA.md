# How to Design Game Mechanics (Beginner to Pro) Part 1 (RookieDev, 5.7 min)

**What it is:** A narrated-slideshow explainer defining "game mechanics" and sorting them into core / secondary / emergent, with a couple of named-game examples per category.
**Substance:** thin: a single three-way taxonomy stretched across 5.5 minutes with generic examples and no worked process, technique, or exercise. Ends on a comment-bait question.

## Ideas, in the video's order
- [0:01:03] Three-way taxonomy: core mechanics (main verb — Tetris rotate/drop, Minecraft mine/craft), secondary mechanics (support systems — Mario power-ups), emergent mechanics (unplanned outcomes of core+secondary combining — The Sims relationships) (generic, but the taxonomy itself is a clean, nameable concept).
- [0:01:30] Core mechanics need to be fun standing alone, or "the whole game could fall apart" (generic).
- [0:02:00] Secondary mechanics add variety without changing the core action (generic).
- [0:02:28] Emergent mechanics "can sometimes be the most interesting part" because they come from system interaction, not design (generic, asserted not shown).
- [0:02:55] Design process is sketched in one sentence: start with a simple idea, test/tweak, add hazards/rewards/variation, aim for "easy to learn, hard to master" (generic).
- [0:03:46] Balance mechanics against each other so they complement rather than clash; cites Dark Souls stamina/health/difficulty as an example of simple-mechanic-made-complex-via-implementation (generic).

## What the frames add
Nothing beyond illustration. Stock/borrowed gameplay clips of Celeste, Portal, Dead Cells, Elden Ring, Super Mario 64, The Sims matched to whatever game is named at that moment, plus on-screen caption text restating the narration verbatim. No original diagram, no worked example beyond naming games.

## For our class (11-13, Minecraft mod, Gemini writes the code)
### Becomes something kids do
- **"Core / secondary / emergent" label pass (10 min)**: for a kid's own mod idea, have them write one core mechanic (the main verb — e.g. "throw fire orb"), one secondary mechanic that supports it (a cooldown, a charge meter), and guess one emergent effect once it's in the real game (e.g. "orbs might set the whole forest on fire"). Cheap vocabulary exercise, not a new insight.

### Survives the move to Minecraft?
The taxonomy transfers cleanly since Minecraft already models it (mine/place = core, potions/enchants = secondary, redstone contraptions = famous real-world emergent behavior) — but it's a labeling exercise, not a design technique; it won't by itself produce a better mechanic.

### Doesn't transfer
Nothing here is Minecraft-specific or actionable beyond the label pass above — there's no process for actually inventing a mechanic, just for categorizing one after the fact.

## Honest caveats
This is the weakest video in the batch alongside its Part 2 sequel: same channel, same slide style, same generic taxonomy-then-named-examples structure with almost no worked example. Don't build more than one small worksheet from this pair combined — there isn't enough substance to justify two separate activities.

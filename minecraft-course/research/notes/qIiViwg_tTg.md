# Exploring Game MECHANICS - Designing a New Board Game (Jesse Ross, 14.1 min)

**What it is:** A small-channel solo designer's dev-vlog, thinking out loud through his own hex-based exploration board game: hex-tile iconography, a random "night phase" die, and personal (non-competitive) victory conditions.
**Substance:** mixed: this is a real, in-progress design process (not a lecture), with genuinely specific choices and physical prototypes shown — but it's narrow (one designer's one board game), rambling, and ends with no resolved system, just brainstormed options.

## Ideas, in the video's order
- [0:01:30]-[0:02:33] An empty hex-crawl map itself creates curiosity/anticipation before any content is added — the "promise" of a mostly-blank explorable map is itself a hook (echoes hbzGO_Qonu0's "mystery/anticipation" point independently).
- [0:04:42] A subscriber's advice he adopts: don't over-invest design effort in the hex grid itself — the "emotion of exploration" comes from what's ON each hex (its content/meaning), not the elegance of the tiling puzzle.
- [0:05:13]-[0:05:44] Small physical space (a hex tile) forces minimal iconography — one terrain + one icon max, or it becomes unreadable at a glance; he cites Tapestry's icon economy as the model.
- [0:06:16]-[0:06:47] A single simple icon (a cave entrance) can imply a whole mini-system (enter, spend turns, risk/reward) without needing to explain it on the tile.
- [0:08:21]-[0:09:24] "Night phase" die roll: a themed random event each night (shooting stars = free resource choice, storm = shared consolation bonus, full moon = bonus turn) — deliberately designed so all players benefit somewhat, not to create advantage/disadvantage.
- [0:09:55]-[0:11:28] Personal, non-competitive victory conditions: each player picks their own goal at setup (reach the mountain, find 3 secrets, "retire" in the woods); goals can overlap without conflict, and completing one is marked publicly (a star/token) so the table sees who's ahead without it being a race.
- [0:11:59] Optional idea: "sightseeing" cards tied to weather/location RNG as an alternate path to a win condition.

## For our class (11-13, Minecraft mod, Gemini writes the code)
### Becomes something kids do
- **"Pick your own win condition" advancement (25 min)**: instead of one fixed mod goal, let each kid define a personal custom advancement for their own mod content (e.g. "tame 3 of my custom mob," "find my custom structure," "survive one night with my custom status effect"). Directly buildable in NeoForge via the advancements system, and it's a natural extension of the game's existing (optional, non-competitive) advancement design.
- **"One icon, one meaning" texture rule (part of the Blockbench/texture session)**: when a kid designs a custom item's 16x16 icon, apply the same discipline as the hex tiles — the icon must read at a glance with no label, same as vanilla Minecraft items already require. A cheap, concrete crit standard for texture review.
- **Design-notebook habit**: encourage kids to sketch mod ideas by hand before opening Blockbench/Gemini, the way this vlogger sketches hexes and camps before building tiles — low-cost, and the visuals above are good reference for what that looks like.

### Survives the move to Minecraft?
The personal-victory-condition idea transfers cleanly (advancements already support this). The "one icon reads at a glance" rule transfers directly, since Minecraft items already work this way. The night-phase die and hex-tile system themselves are board-game mechanics with no direct Minecraft equivalent — but the underlying idea (a themed random event that always gives everyone something, never just a penalty) could inform a custom weather effect or "blessed night" mechanic.

### Doesn't transfer
The video is fundamentally about physical board-game production (hex tile printing, dice manufacturing, laser cutting) — none of that hardware/prototyping content applies. It's also unfinished and unresolved (he explicitly says he has "steps two through one hundred" left), so there's no complete system to hand kids as a model, only fragments.

## Honest caveats
This is a small solo channel (100 subscribers per his own count at the time) thinking out loud, not a polished design lecture — treat every claim as one designer's untested opinion, not proven design wisdom. Several segments are just him talking to camera with no new information (padding); the ideas above are the only substantive extractable content across 14 minutes.

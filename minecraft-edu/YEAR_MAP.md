## 1. Grade 6 science: what's actually required

The year is 120 hours. 80% of it is required core content and 20% is optional enrichment. The Ministry recommends
building learning around one big question or challenge, which suits quests well.

| Topic | Hours | Required? | How well it fits Minecraft |
|---|---|---|---|
| Energy: types, conversions, transfers | 6 | yes | ★★★ Redstone is electricity, and machines show conversions |
| **Light OR sound** (the teacher picks one) + the eye OR the ear to match | 12 + 6 + 3 | yes | Sound ★★★ (note blocks = pitch, sculk sensors detect vibrations, wool blocks vibrations). Light ★★ (needs a mod for colours and refraction) |
| Electrical energy, energy resources, power plants | 4 + 2 + 6 | yes | ★★★ Build a power station: fuel → steam → turbine → generator |
| Electricity and the environment, climate | 4 | yes | ★★★ "Where should we build the power station?" on a map with a village, a forest and a river |
| Habitats: what makes Earth livable (water, oxygen, temperature) | 2 | yes | ★★★ Earth versus a Mars world where you need oxygen |
| Diversity and adaptations (desert, camel, duck…) | 8 | yes | ★★★ Biomes and real animals in the game (camel, fox, frog by biome) |
| Interactions: competition, mutualism, parasitism, food webs | 4 + 10 | yes | ★★★ Food chains already exist in the game (wolf→sheep, fox→chicken); remove a predator and watch what happens (a small mod) |
| Human impact: deforestation, nature reserves | 4 | yes | ★★★ Clear a forest and see the cost |
| Body: heart and blood, body as a system | — | enrichment | ★★★ A heartbeat that speeds up when you sprint, so kids measure their "pulse" in the game |
| Technological system (input → process → output) | 6 | yes | ★★★ An automatic crop-watering system is literally one of the spec's own examples |
| Production: handcraft versus industrial | — | enrichment | ★★★ The class builds an assembly line together, one station per kid |
| Inquiry (15h) **or** engineering design (15h) | 15 | yes (grade 5 or 6) | ★★★ The core of every world: measure in the game, write up, draw a conclusion |

**MITZAV:** there's a grade 6 science exam on 31.5.2027, covering the required material for grades 4–6:
ecology 25%, materials 20%, living systems 20%, energy 15%, astronomy 10%, technology 10%. "Prepares for MITZAV" is a
sentence that sells to principals. Ecology is the biggest chunk.

## 2. English at the end of grade 6

- The target level is **A1 (Basic User I)**, and the vocabulary is **Band I Core II** (the official list is in the folder).
- The can-dos that fit a game naturally:
  - follow short, simple instructions and directions ("go to the red door", "take three apples")
  - understand short descriptions of animals, places and objects (colour, size, location)
  - ask and answer "What's this?" and "Where is…?" using in, on, under and next to
  - numbers, quantities and prices; follow pictured instructions for making something
  - write lists and short messages (a collection list, a note)
- Band I already has much of what we need: animal, plant, tree, water, sun, wind, forest, eat, ear, heart,
  sound, noise, dark… Scientific words (energy, oxygen, habitat) aren't in Band I. So in English we teach the
  everyday words around the topic, and the science terms stay in Hebrew.

**How it works in the game:** everything is in Hebrew except one character, **an English-speaking villager**, who
appears in every world. They give tasks, trade and describe things in Band I English only. To finish their quests,
the kid has to understand: "Bring me two animals that live in the desert." That way science and English sit in the
same quest instead of two half-lessons.

## 3. The worlds (the year map)

Each world is a single map with one big question. A world is a few meetings, not a single lesson.

| # | World | The big question | Science | English (with the villager) | What we build |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The living island** | "Why do some animals live only here?" | habitats, adaptations, food chains and webs, interactions, human impact | animals, colours, sizes, eat/live/need, in/on/under | Mostly vanilla + datapack (quests, a predator-removal experiment); a small mod for an ecosystem meter |
| 2 | **Mars versus Earth** | "What would we need to live there?" | conditions for life (water, oxygen, temperature) | weather, hot/cold, lists ("I need…") | A Mars dimension with an oxygen meter (Java) |
| 3 | **The quiet city** (or "the glass city" if light) | "How do you build a home where no one hears the noise?" | sound: pitch, loudness, travel through materials, absorption, echo; the ear and noise health | loud/quiet, high/low, numbers (noise readings) | A sound meter + the sculk sensor's vibration readings; a giant walk-through ear |
| 4 | **The power station** | "How do we light up the village without burning the forest?" | energy conversions, generators, fuel vs renewable, climate, choosing a site | directions, quantities, "Let's…" | Turbine, generator and pollution meter blocks (Java); redstone as the grid |
| 5 | **The automatic farm** | "Build a machine that solves a problem" | technological system, input-process-output, engineering design, assembly line | follow pictured making instructions, sequence words (first, next, then) | Mostly redstone; challenge cards |
| 6 | **Inside the body** (enrichment) | "What happens to the heart when you run?" | heart and blood, body as a system, pulse | body parts, feelings, I'm tired/happy | A pulse meter that rises with sprinting (Java); a giant heart to walk through |

Inquiry runs through every world: a research question, a hypothesis, what we change and what we keep fixed, repeats,
a table and a conclusion, all inside the game.

## 4. The kit every world shares

This is what makes us different from Minecraft Education, which has science worlds but no real measuring.

- **Measuring tools:** thermometer, sound meter, light meter, pulse meter, ecosystem counter (how many animals of each
  kind). They show a number, and the number can be written down.
- **Research notebook:** an in-game book with a ready template (question, hypothesis, what I change, what stays fixed,
  results table, conclusion). The teacher collects it at the end.
- **The English villager:** quests, trading and descriptions in Band I English only.
- **A teacher remote:** freeze everyone, teleport the class to a spot, reset the world. (Minecraft Education has
  this; schools will expect it.)
- **Tech:** ready-made worlds + datapacks where possible; Java only for the tools and special blocks. That keeps it
  less fragile and quicker to build.

## 5. Three decisions for you

**A. Light or sound?** The teacher chooses one of the two for the whole year. I recommend **sound**. The game already
has pitch (note blocks), vibrations (sculk sensors) and materials that block them (wool), so it's quicker to build and
more natural for kids. Light needs a mod for colours and refraction. Later we can build both so every school finds a
match.

💬 BEN:

**B. Which world first?** I recommend **world 1, the living island**. It's the biggest MITZAV chunk (25%), the game
already has animals, biomes and food, it uses the most Band I English words, and it's the quickest to build. The power
station is the second option: more impressive in a demo, but more Java.

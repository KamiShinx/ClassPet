# Exercise types seen in the released tests and tasks (for porting to Minecraft and a workbook)

Source: text I extracted and read from the downloaded government files (public RAMA / Ministry documents).
I read in full or in large part: the grade 6 English test 2025 (`mitzav/eng6-2025-test.pdf`), the grade 5 science test 2014 (`sci5-2014-test.doc`),
and the grade 6 science units "light and the eye" (`sci6-unit-energy-radiation-task.doc`) and "electricity" (`sci6-unit-electric-energy-task.doc`).
Other files in the folder were NOT read; anything I say about them is from their titles. Paraphrased examples only.

Other (from titles, not read): performance tasks such as "What will we eat at summer camp" (food groups), "A new record in demand for electricity", "A pot as a gift" (materials),
"Trip to the Moon", "Kingfishers", "Tomatoes in the Negev" (ecology / agriculture), "What is a bike light made of", "Let's save the sea turtles", and the ecosystem units
"interrelationships in an ecosystem", "ecosystems", "life characteristics" (`mitzav/sci6-*`, `mitzav/sci56-perf-*`).

Common thread: nearly every item starts from a small story (a named child, a news clip, a device) and asks for a cause, a prediction, a choice with a reason, or a fair-test analysis.
The three recurring verbs are explain, justify, and identify the variable.

## Porting notes (my view, not a source claim)

- Items 6, 12, 13 in science and 2, 5, 10 in English map straight onto a Minecraft build-and-record loop: a world gives the data (a graph on a sign board, a table in a chest),
  the kid fills a table in a book-and-quill, chooses and justifies.
- Items "circle the picture" and "match picture to word" are the cheapest to port (item frames and signs).
- The weekly-calendar instruction task is a good template for a reading-and-doing exercise inside the game.

# English for the game: what the English villager may ask, in which words, and how we check it

For the grade 6 science world. Everything in the game is in Hebrew except one English-speaking villager who gives
quests in simple English. The end-of-grade-6 target is **A1 (Basic User I)**, with vocabulary from **Band I (Core I +
Core II)**.

Sources (Ministry of Education, all in `minecraft-edu/curriculum/english/`):
- `curriculum/eng-curriculum-2020-elementary.pdf`: the A1 can-dos (pp. 27–35), Table 6 (grammar by grade, pp. 36–39),
  the grammar can-dos (pp. 40–45), the lexical bands (pp. 18–19), and the principles for tasks and assessment (pp. 12–13)
- `web/eng-can-dos-basic-user-i-a1-foundation.md`: the current web version of the A1 can-dos (it adds a few)
- `vocabulary/eng-lexical-band1.pdf` (Pre-Band I, Core I, Core II, with Rec/Prod, **updated 26 Jan 2023**) and
  `vocabulary/eng-lexical-band1-elementary.xlsx` (the **Sept 2019** Excel, the only one with an **Expansion** tab);
  `eng-lexical-band2.pdf` / `band3.pdf` were used to find where non-Band I words sit

## 0. Word tags, and a surprise about the two word lists

**The two official lists disagree.** The 2019 Excel and the 2023 PDF are both Ministry files, but the PDF (the later
one, "typos and slight errors corrected") moved dozens of words between bands. Examples: *wild, stone, skin, health,
heat, fresh, check, share, nothing, angry, carry, measure, funny, terrible, interested* are Core I/II in the 2019
Excel, but in the 2023 PDF they are **Band II**. Going the other way, *river, island, storm, snake, knee, plastic,
leather, square, cookie, onion, camera* appear only in the 2023 PDF's Band I. **I treat the 2023 PDF as the
authority** for Pre-Band I, Core I and Core II, and the 2019 Excel only for the Expansion list (the PDF has none).

The curriculum says what each tier means (p. 18): Pre-Band I = grade 3 to the start of grade 4; Core I = grade 4 to
mid-grade 5; Core II = mid-grade 5 to the end of grade 6; Expansion = optional, "at the teacher's discretion". Targets
for A1: 1,200 receptive words and 1,000 productive (cumulative with Pre-Band I: 1,400 / 1,200).

| Tag | Meaning | What the game may do with it |
|---|---|---|
| `Pre` | Pre-Band I (assumed known by grade 6) | use freely |
| `C1` / `C2` | Band I Core I / Core II (2023 PDF) | use freely |
| `…r` (e.g. `C1r`) | the PDF marks it **Receptive** only | the villager may say or write it; don't make the kid spell or say it |
| no `r` | the PDF marks it **Productive** | the kid may be asked to write or say it, and spelling can be checked (curriculum: "can spell the productive entries of Band I with reasonable accuracy") |
| `Exp` | Band I Expansion (2019 Excel only) | OK in villager text; never required |
| `x19` | was Core I/II in the 2019 Excel, but is Band II in the 2023 PDF | gloss it or show a picture; never required |
| `B2` / `B3` | not Band I; it's in Band II / Band III | gloss (picture or Hebrew tooltip), or say it in Hebrew |
| `—` | not a headword in Bands I–III | same as B2/B3 |

Rules of thumb that follow from this:
- A word can be in Band I with only one meaning. *long* is Band I only in its **time** meaning (`C2r`); its length
  meaning is only in the 2019 Excel. *kind* is Band I only as a **noun** ("what kind of", `C2r`); *kind* = nice is
  **B2**. *light* is in three times: not heavy (`C1`), brightness or lamp (`C2`), pale (`C2r`).
- Family members count. The PDF lists *wooden* under *wood*, *cloudy* under *cloud*, *surprised* under *surprise*,
  and *teeth*, *feet* and *children* as irregular plurals.
- **Corrections for `YEAR_MAP.md`:** *loud* is B2 (use *noisy* `C2r` / *quiet* `C2r`), *desert* is B2, *habitat* is
  in no band, and "Be kind" uses the B2 sense. So "Bring me two animals that live in the desert" needs *desert* glossed
  (or "…that live in a hot, dry place": *hot* `Pre`, *dry* `C1`, *place* `C1r`).

## 1. Can-dos and grammar the villager can practise

Mode key: **L** = listening (the villager's audio plus its subtitle), **R** = reading (signs, books, quest text),
**W** = writing (a book and quill in the game, or the workbook), **S** = spoken interaction in class (pairs, teacher,
role-play around the game). Can-do wording is shortened from the curriculum; "(web)" means it is only in the current
web version.

### 1a. Listening (spoken reception)

| Can-do (A1) | Quest pattern |
|---|---|
| Understand and follow short, simple instructions/directions | "Go to the big tree. Turn left. Open the red door." |
| Act on instructions that involve times, locations, numbers | "Come back at night with three fish." |
| Understand an instruction with a condition (web) | "If the animal can fly, put it in the blue box." |
| Understand where something is, when it's in the immediate environment | "The key is under the bed, next to the window." |
| Recognise key words in short descriptions of objects, people, animals | "It is small and white. It lives on the farm. It gives us eggs. Bring it to me." |
| Understand basic phrases about the weather | "It's cold and windy today. Bring me a coat." |
| Pick out concrete information (places, times) from short audio | A voice message: "Meet me at the river in the morning." |
| Identify day, date and time in short dialogues | "The market opens on Monday at eight o'clock." |
| Understand basic descriptors (Core I/II adjectives) about where things or people are | "The horse is behind the old house, near the water." |
| Follow a short familiar story, with pictures | The villager tells a 6-line story with pictures, then asks: "What did the cat eat?" |
| Understand teacher feedback in simple language | Villager feedback lines: "Well done!" / "That's right!" / "No, that's wrong. Try again." |
| Seek repetition | An **"Again, please"** button that replays the line more slowly (Pre-Band I has "Say it again please") |

### 1b. Reading (written reception)

| Can-do (A1) | Quest pattern |
|---|---|
| Read familiar words and chunks (decode) | Labelled boxes and signs: "APPLES", "WATER", "FARM →" |
| Understand sentences describing appearance and everyday items (colour, size) | "Find the tall tree with white flowers." |
| Get the idea of simple informational material, with pictures | A field-guide page: "Cows eat grass. They drink a lot of water." Then the kid fills in a fact file (§3) |
| Understand short messages (postcard, note, email) | A note in a box: "Dear friend, I am at the farm. Come at noon. Bring bread." |
| Find key information in ads, programmes, posters (what, cost, date, place) | A village poster: "Fish market. Tuesday, 10 o'clock. 2 fish = 1 apple." |
| Read a simple timetable of days and times | The villager's week on a board: "Monday: farm. Tuesday: river." "Where is he on Tuesday?" |
| Follow basic instructions for making something, with pictures | A crafting card: "First put three wood on the table. Then…" (world 5) |
| Understand written classroom instructions ("Read and match") | Quest text reuses the same instruction verbs: *Read and match / Read and colour / Circle / Write* |
| Follow short, simple written directions from X to Y (web) | A trail of signs: "Go up the hill. Turn right at the big stone…" (gloss *stone*) |
| Find proper names by their capital letters | Map signs: "Big River", "Red Hill". "Which one is a place?" |

### 1c. Writing (written production and written interaction)

| Can-do (A1) | Quest pattern |
|---|---|
| Write lists for a purpose, from memory or with pictures | "Write a list: five things a cow needs." |
| Describe everyday objects (colour, size), given a model | "Write two sentences about your house: It is ___. It is ___." |
| Write what someone has (things, pets), given a model | "My farm has three cows and two sheep." |
| Write about simple daily routines | An observation log: "What does the cat do in the morning? At night?" |
| Write short, simple texts (card, invitation, short message) | A thank-you note to the villager; a note "At the river. Back at 5." |
| Write basic information on a form | An animal card: Name / Colour / Lives in / Eats |
| Spell the productive Band I words reasonably | A lock that opens when the kid writes the word on a sign (productive words only) |
| Post short positive or negative reactions with set phrases | Comments on a classmate's build: "Great house!" "I like the red roof." |

### 1d. Spoken interaction and production (in class, around the game)

| Can-do (A1) | Quest pattern |
|---|---|
| Ask and answer simple questions about objects and pictures (colour, size, location) | Pairs: "Where is the horse?" "It's behind the house." |
| Ask people for things and give them things | Trading role-play: "Can I have two apples, please?" "Here you are." |
| Ask "What's this?" and understand a 1–2 word answer | A guessing game with spawn eggs or blocks |
| Describe the position of objects | Information gap: one kid describes a hidden build, the partner builds it |
| Say what the weather is like | The opening "weather report": "Today is rainy and cold." |
| Exchange likes and dislikes | "Do you like cats or dogs?" A quick class survey |
| Say what someone's job is | The villagers' jobs: farmer, doctor, teacher, cook, driver (all Band I) |
| Draw simple conclusions about people in pictures ("he's happy") | The villager's face changes: "How does he feel?" "He's sad." |
| Express immediate needs ("I need a pen") | "I need water." "I need three wood." |
| Ask for repetition and for spelling | "Say it again, please." "How do you spell it?" |
| Creative play: chants, tongue twisters, games with fixed phrases | The villager's daily tongue twister; a guessing game with fixed questions |
| **Mediation:** pass on, in Hebrew, simple information from English signs and notices | One kid reads the English sign, then tells the team in Hebrew what it says |

### 1e. Grammar (Table 6, the grade 6 column)

Levels for grade 6: **E** = evolving usage and accuracy (can be required), **EP** = emerging production (encourage,
don't mark down), **R** = mostly receptive (only in the villager's lines).

| Structure | Gr 6 | Quest pattern |
|---|---|---|
| *to be*, present (affirmative, negative, yes/no) | E | "Is it big? Yes, it is." / "The fish isn't in the box." |
| Wh-questions + *be* + prepositions of location | E | "Where is the ___?" "It's in/on/under/behind/between/next to the ___." |
| *There is / There are* | E | "There are three ___ in the ___. Find them." / "How many cows are there?" |
| Adjective before the noun | E | "Bring me a big red flower." |
| Imperatives, affirmative and negative | E | Every quest: "Take…", "Go…", "Don't cut the trees!" |
| *Let's* + verb | E | "Let's build a house for the horse." |
| Present simple (facts, habits) | EP | "Cows eat grass. What do sheep eat?" |
| *can* (ability) | E (affirmative), EP (negative, questions) | "Which animal can fly? Can a pig swim?" |
| Past *be* with wh-questions | E | "Where was the cat yesterday?" |
| Articles a/an/the | E | "Take an apple. Give the apple to the horse." |
| Present progressive (all forms) | E | "Look! What is the cow doing?" "It's eating." |
| Past simple (regular, Band I irregulars) | EP | "What did you find?" "I found two eggs." |
| Possessive *'s* and possessive pronouns | EP | "Bring the farmer's dog. Where is his hat?" |
| Present simple vs progressive | EP | "Cows eat grass. Now the cow is drinking water." |
| *going to* (plans) | EP | Before a build: "What are you going to build?" |
| Modals of request and obligation | R | "Can you help me?" "You must close the door." |
| Comparatives and superlatives | R | "Which is bigger, the cow or the cat?" "Find the tallest tree." |
| Stative verbs as chunks (*think, know, want, like*) | R | "I think the horse is hungry." |
| Count/non-count quantifiers | R | "Bring me some water and a lot of eggs." "How many?" / "How much?" |
| Reported speech (no backshift) | R | "The farmer said that the horse is hungry." |
| Adverbs of manner | R | "Walk slowly. Be quiet." |
| *will* | R | "I will give you a key." |
| *because*; *and / but / or* (grammar can-dos, A1) | E | "Why do fish live in water? Because…" / "Bring me a cow or a sheep." |
| Plurals, including the irregular *feet, teeth, children* | E | "Count the feet: how many feet has a chicken got?" |
| Prepositions of time and place | E | "Come in the morning." "Meet me on Monday." |

## 3. Exercise types in the Ministry lesson files

What the Ministry's own elementary units actually do, and how each becomes a villager task and a printed workbook
item. (`HowFitAreYou` is a junior-high unit at Bands I+II/B1; I used it only for its formats.) Short file names: W3 =

## 4. How the Ministry files assess A1

**The curriculum's principles (p. 13):** use multiple methods over time; learners know the criteria in advance;
self- and peer-assessment; "rubrics, assessment lists and checklists can be used to evaluate learners' performance
of oral and written tasks"; keep test anxiety low. The Ministry also keeps a bank of **performance tasks with
rubrics** (the elementary programmes page); four of them are in `lessons/` (Tasks 91, 93, 94, 95).

What the files actually use:

1. **Holistic 5-point rubrics for performance tasks** (Tasks 91, 93, 94). Descriptors are written for 5, 3 and 1;
   4 and 2 fall in between. The criteria repeat across tasks:
   - fluency or hesitation
   - English only, or L1 used often
   - clear or unclear speech
   - vocabulary varied, basic or limited
   - word order and verb forms
   - and, for dialogues, whether content and interaction were appropriate

   Task 93 adds a separate **Understanding** column (understood and answered the questions). Every task says: "It
   is very important to teach pupils how to use a checklist before they are given this performance task."
2. **Two-part writing rubric** (Task 95, the thank-you note). Part 1 is **Planning** (filled in the blanks; "do not
   take off points for spelling and grammar in Part One"). Part 2 is **Performance** (number of sentences, required
   elements, organisation, vocabulary, and spelling, word order, plurals, verb forms, capitals and punctuation). It
   comes with a pupil-facing scale from "Good start" to "Well done!".
3. **4-point analytic rubric** (`Letsshareandbefriends`): labelling, title and caption, understanding of the story,
   writing (2–3 sentences with target words), language use (spelling, capitals, punctuation). It's paired with a
   **pupil writing checklist** ("I drew a picture… I gave it a title… I labelled it with correct words… I wrote a
   short sentence… I used correct spelling, capital letters and full stops").
4. **Can-do checklists for self-assessment** ("I can understand familiar words and phrases spoken slowly…", "I can
   write short, clear sentences…": `Letsshareandbefriends` Exit Ticket B; Football self-assessment Yes/No). The
   wording is the curriculum's can-dos turned into "I can". Helping Others adds a reflection list ("I felt good
   about the task / I worked well with a partner / I need to work more on: reading, listening, writing, speaking,
   vocabulary").
5. **Peer-evaluation checklists** for presentations (`oneminuteofpeace`: shared at least 3 tips, used new words,
   capital letters, grammar and spelling, pictures and colours).
6. **Exit tickets**: 3-2-1 (W3, W6, G5, Num: 3 things I learned, 2 tasks I liked, 1 question); yes/no comprehension
   (Lets share Exit A); "write six new words on the star" (`friends`).
7. **Quick formative checks**: green YES / red NO cards held up for teacher statements (Maccabiah), plus a **teacher
   reflection** against the lesson objectives (Big Umbrella, Giving Tree, Lets share).

**What this means for us:**
- **Receptive items are checked by the game.** These are types 1–7, 9, 10, 11 and 13 in §3. Log each kid's result
  per can-do. That's the "evidence over time" the curriculum asks for, at no cost to the teacher.
- **Productive items are judged by the teacher** with the Ministry's own instruments. Writing (lists, notes, fact
  files) gets the 4-point analytic rubric plus the pupil checklist. Speaking (guessing game, trading role-play,
  information gap) gets the 5-point holistic rubric. Only **productive (P)** Band I words are spell-checked.
- **Each world ends with an "I can" page** in the workbook: 4–6 can-dos from §1 in English, with Hebrew support,
  ticked by the kid (the Ministry format), plus a 3-2-1 exit ticket.
- Teach the checklist before the task, as the Ministry insists, by showing the rubric on screen at the start of the world.

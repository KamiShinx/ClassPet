# T03 summary — Simplilearn short (<1h) UI/UX videos

**57 videos triaged.** core: 7, useful: 3, skip: 47 (9 of the skips were `nocaps`, judged from title only —
all matched patterns already confirmed skip-worthy from captioned duplicates: career roadmaps, Figma plugin
listicles, AI-tool listicles, a bootcamp ad).

The channel is almost entirely three genres, and the brief's instinct to be strict was correct: **Figma/Canva/Adobe
XD/InVision click-by-click tutorials** (by far the largest bucket — mockups, prototyping, animation, grid systems,
responsive design, dashboards, plugins, Figma-to-HTML), **career content** (roadmaps, salaries, interview Q&A,
portfolio/Behance building — two near-identical "UX/UI Interview Questions" videos, three near-identical
"roadmap"/"how to become" videos), and **AI-tool listicles** (product tours with pricing/pros-cons, no actual
concept of what AI contributes to design — none of the AI-tools videos earned better than skip). None of the
outdated tool tutorials transfer to a course where Gemini writes the code and kids never open Figma.

**The 5 best videos:**
1. **ODpB9-MCa5s — "UI/UX Explained In 8 Minutes"** — the standout. Tells the entire design process (research →
   wireframe → visual design → info architecture → usability testing) as a story about a student, Sam, building a
   homework app. No career pitch, minimal tool-naming, fully teen-followable.
2. **n5UsQb6OnPU — "12 Laws Of UI/UX Design"** — dense, accurate rundown of the classic named UX laws (Fitts's,
   Hick's, Jakob's, Miller's, Pareto, von Restorff, etc.), each with a real named-app example. Great reference.
3. **WwroSqXjrLM — "What Is Visual Hierarchy?"** — seven concrete principles (size, contrast, perspective,
   typography, balance, proximity, reading patterns) with Tesla/Nike/Netflix examples. Directly applicable.
4. **TqsYDUBoIxQ — "Color Theory In UI Design"** — clean, complete color-wheel/harmony/contrast/psychology
   explainer, self-contained and correct.
5. **COTOz4XmhWU — "What Is Human Centered Design?"** — the six-phase HCD process with the IDEO nurse-device and
   Uber examples; useful for framing how students should approach their own project's users.

Rounding out `core`: **08MrVhy2qk8** (7 UI principles: hierarchy, progressive disclosure, consistency, contrast,
accessibility, proximity, alignment) and **xbcZrENvGd8** (5-phase design sprint, maps well onto structuring project
weeks).

**`useful` (not for kids directly, but worth the teacher's eye):** **rLdpn0vHV-M**'s first 7 minutes (wireframe
concept/fidelity, before it collapses into 25 min of Figma clicking), **1fMeovdfCeU** (solid UX overview, mostly a
duplicate of ODpB9-MCa5s), and **zTElKDsC9Fw** (a project-idea bank — todo app, fitness dashboard, user personas,
usability testing — good for scoping student projects even though framed around job portfolios).

**Duplicates noticed:** at least 4 near-identical "what is UI/UX" intros (55NvZjUZIO8, 1fMeovdfCeU, R5AIY0EP7QA,
ODpB9-MCa5s — only the last earns core); 2 identical-format "interview questions" videos; 3 "roadmap" videos; 2
"create mockups in Figma" videos; the AI-tools listicles are basically the same video re-shot yearly (2023/2024/2026
versions of "Top N AI Tools").

**Surprising:** the two most substantive-sounding tool videos — "Responsive Design in Figma" and "Grid Systems in
Figma" — both open with a genuinely correct explanation of a transferable concept (responsive breakpoints, the
4pt/8pt spacing system) in the first 1-2 minutes, then spend 15+ minutes on pure Figma mechanics. Not worth
including for that ratio, but worth knowing the concepts exist if a future guide-writer wants to explain grid
spacing conventions from scratch rather than by video.

# T06 summary — CareerFoundry intro-to-UX playlist

**30 videos: 7 core, 1 useful, 22 skip.**

This playlist is CareerFoundry's YouTube marketing funnel for their paid UX bootcamp, and it shows: roughly
three-quarters of it is career content — portfolio tips, portfolio *reviews*, interview questions, hiring-manager
breakdowns, "day in the life," networking/LinkedIn, "how I'd learn UX in 2024," and six short vox-pop compilations
(designers giving 2-minute hot takes on tools/AI/companies/career advice) that carry almost no teachable content.
None of that fits a course about vibe-coding apps with Gemini.

## The 5 best videos

1. **What Is Information Architecture?** (`OJLfjgVlwDo`) — the strongest video in the group. Live-demos mental
   models, cognitive load and visual hierarchy on the real Amazon site (search, filters, category structure). Maps
   directly onto how kids should plan an app's screens and navigation before asking Gemini to build it.
2. **The UX Design Process For Beginners** (`XhPQbUsD9c0`) — the 5-stage design-thinking cycle (understand → analyze
   → ideate → validate → iterate), taught through a genuinely good real example (how Uber found and fixed its two
   biggest pain points). Clean and teen-followable.
3. **How To Create Your First Wireframe** (`qpH7-KFWZRI`) — hand-sketches a login screen, then rebuilds it in a free
   tool in real time. Exactly the "plan before you build" habit the course wants; short and hands-on.
4. **UX Research - Qualitative User Research** (`bAARmsv1tms`) — a practical, zero-budget guide to interviewing 5
   people, with concrete good-vs-bad ("leading") question examples. Directly usable for the "describe and test" step
   of their workflow.
5. **A Beginner's Guide To UX Writing** (`hsMLqUFtPbU`) — the first 33 minutes (it's a 60-min webinar) is a strong,
   example-rich lesson on button/error/menu text, anchored by a real disaster (the 2018 Hawaii missile-alert UI
   error) and a critique of five real 404 pages. The back third is a live chat-reading writing exercise that needs a
   guide to use.

Also **core**: the intro video `ziQEqGZB8GE` (relatable Norman-doors/toaster examples of good vs bad UX) and
`2yVZjPbqs-4` on inclusive design/accessibility (concrete before/after screenshots — colorblind patterns, icon
choices, gender fields — though at 41 min and touching identity/bias topics it needs a facilitator).

**Useful only**: `vyWFS4DPpqA` (AI for UX design) has a solid segment on AI pitfalls (bias, over-reliance, prompt
quality) worth mining for a teacher-led AI-literacy discussion, but its tool demos (ChatGPT/Midjourney/Notion AI)
don't map onto the kids' Gemini-only workflow.

## Duplicates / patterns noticed

- **Two nearly identical "UX Design Trends" videos** (2022's `aFJpdHEvR64` and 2023's `9aYM_yavyCw`) — both dated
  speculation (metaverse, web3, super-apps); 9aYM's one useful nugget (content design/UX writing) is done far better
  and hands-on in `hsMLqUFtPbU`.
- **Two near-identical portfolio-review videos** (`ePiz3jtLk-k`, `4aMLu1pRa-g`) — same format, same host, same
  "hiring manager critiques 3 portfolios" structure.
- **Six short vox-pop compilations** (best tools, best resources, best companies, "nobody knows how AI will
  impact UX," "is it too late," "quickest ways to become a UX designer") are all the same format — a handful of
  designers giving 15-second opinions, cut together — and none of them teach anything a kid could use.

## Surprising

The channel's actual concept teaching (UX process, IA, research, writing, wireframing) is genuinely solid and
well-illustrated with real screenshots when it shows up — the problem is it's a small fraction of a channel built
to funnel career-changers into a paid bootcamp, so triage here is mostly about filtering out the career noise.

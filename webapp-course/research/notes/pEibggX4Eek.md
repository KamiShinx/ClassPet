# UX Design - What is it? (AJ&Smart, 21.0 min, 2019)

**What it is / substance:** A talking-head video (not a design tutorial) about how "UX designer" as a job title was
being replaced by "product designer" in Silicon Valley circa 2019, illustrated by reading through real job postings
from Google, Twitter, Facebook, and a few smaller/UK companies.
**Is it good enough to assign to a 14-year-old as homework?** No. This is a 2019 tech-industry hiring/job-market
video for adults job-hunting or hiring, not a design-skills lesson. It teaches zero concrete UI/UX techniques,
vocabulary, or visual before/afters. Not relevant to what this batch needs (kids learning UI/UX basics to describe
to Gemini). Skip it for the library; there's nothing here worth a homework page.

**Watch-list:** None recommended — no segment teaches a reusable design skill.

## The ideas (in order, with [h:mm:ss])
- [0:00:33]-[0:01:38] Reads two "official" definitions of UX design (Interaction Design Foundation, Wikipedia) and
  dismisses them as too abstract/unpractical.
- [0:02:10]-[0:04:20] Claim: in 2019, big companies had stopped hiring "UX designers" and split the role into
  narrower titles: UX engineer, visual designer, motion designer, interaction designer, UX researcher, UX writer,
  content strategist. Argues no one person realistically does all of these.
- [0:04:51]-[0:10:52] Walks through a real Twitter "Senior Product Designer" job listing line by line, highlighting
  the phrase "identify user needs, sketch solutions, prototype, test ideas with user research, refine with data and
  feedback" [0:07:35] as, in his view, the best one-sentence description of what UX/product design actually is.
- [0:11:58]-[0:14:38] Same exercise with a Facebook "Product Designer" listing; notes UX research is again a
  separate role there.
- [0:15:13]-[0:17:58] Contrasts these with smaller/older-style UK job ads (Anglian Water, a startup, BBC) that lump
  UX+UI+research+SEO together, which he calls old-school and poorly scoped.
- [0:19:05]-[0:20:09] Conclusion/thesis: "what UX design is, is what the market decides" — i.e. definitions should
  be read off real job postings, not textbooks, and the trend is UX-design-as-a-title splitting into "product
  designer" (design) + "UX researcher" (research) as separate jobs.

## Vocabulary for prompting Gemini
None. This video is about job titles and hiring trends, not about any term a kid would use to describe UI to an AI
(no mention of cards, modals, toasts, hierarchy, affordance, spacing, contrast, etc.).

## Before/after examples from the frames
None. All 10 contact sheets show either the presenter talking to camera (color and a black-and-white "cutaway"
segment used purely for visual variety) or screen recordings of job-posting web pages / bullet-point text overlays
quoting those postings. There is no app UI, no design tool, no code, and no before/after design comparison anywhere
in the video. The frames add nothing for this library's purposes.

## Caveats
- Entirely career/hiring content, not a skills tutorial — mismatched with this batch's brief (UI basics, UX basics,
  user flows as design skills).
- From 2019: the specific claim that "UX designer" job titles were being replaced by "product designer" reflects a
  2019 Silicon Valley hiring trend the presenter was speculating about; treat as dated industry commentary, not a
  design principle, and don't repeat the prediction as settled fact.
- No visuals, no code, no app screens, no design demo of any kind — contact sheets confirm the entire video is
  either the presenter talking or static job-listing text.
- Recommend excluding this video from the U1 library page entirely rather than forcing it into the page structure.
